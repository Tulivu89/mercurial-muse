# Bogwater

The frame for Bogwater in the eel run: nine places, twelve people who matter, four threads on clocks that meet on sitting day, and a runaway with a contract inked on their skin. Everything here is a Suggestion built on the Fenmire, Woed, Cairn Maw and Port Covenant docs, written in plain words so nobody needs a glossary.

## The runaway

The runaway walked off a contract in Port Covenant, and the Pact punished it with an indenture inked into the skin (Port Covenant doc). It runs down the inside of the forearm, and anyone who reads the Arbiters' hand can read what the runaway owes and to whom.

The fen reads that ink two ways. It despises owning people, and slavers caught there don't leave. It also hates the colony, distrusts outsiders and kills trespassers (Owner). Every fen face the runaway meets decides which of those it means.

**Two states, not one.** The ink and the Pact's claim are separate, and freedom means ending the claim.

| State | Bands | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The claim: the Arbiters' copy, carried by Orlo | Owed (start), Released, Closed | While Owed, the patrol and Orlo hunt the runaway, and anyone can earn a reward for them |
| The mark: the ink on the arm | Inked (start), Scarred | While Inked, anyone who sees the arm knows. Fen folk start a step colder, and the patrol knows the runaway on sight |

**Routes to freedom** are the module's spine. Each costs something different, and any one can end it.

| Route | How | Sets | What it costs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Buy it out | 100 coin to the Arbiters. Edric, who trades at Port Covenant too, carries the coin and brings the release back | Claim to Released | The most money in the module, Edric's cut, and trusting him with it |
| Die on paper | Orlo or the patrol watches a burning on the burning ground and signs off the death | Claim to Closed | A body that passes, Meyrick's word, and never being seen by the colony again. If Orlo sees the runaway alive, the claim is Owed again |
| Burn it off | Morwen takes the ink out with lye and a hot knife | Mark to Scarred | A serious injury, a scar the shape of what was there, and Morwen's price: tamecap from the Woed's edge |
| A forged release | Hard Focus work in the Arbiters' hand, copied from any Pact paper. The sergeant carries his orders | Claim to Released, until someone checks | A despair, or Orlo reading it, puts the claim back to Owed and the runaway in front of him |
| Run again (not freedom) | Out with the Low Road through Cadfan, in an eel barrel to Caerwyn | Leaves the module | 20 coin. The ink goes too, and the Low Road sometimes sells its passengers on (Societies doc) |

Scarred on its own isn't freedom, but the patrol can no longer know the runaway on sight.

**The default test character,** which the player can change: a human who walked off a shipwright's indenture and has been running for two days. A human gets the fen's coldest welcome, so the save tests it hardest. They start bruised and strained, with no coin and a knife. Finesse 3 Trained, Force 2 and Focus 2 Practiced, Flair 1 Raw: the fen won't be talked round easily, which tests Focus, bribes and every other way around a weak approach.

## The opening

The module's opening text, at the long fence an hour before dawn on day 1. The narrator copies its voice more than any rule, so it is written in the house format: narration in asterisks, speech plain.

```markdown
*The net comes up out of the black water all at once, and you come up with it. Eels slide over your arms and down your collar, cold and thick and still fighting, and the cord bites into the backs of your knees. A lantern swings out on a pole and stops a hand from your face.*

*Three salamanders crouch on the plank walk along the top of the fence, black hides beaded with wet, orange blotches gone dull in the lantern light. The nearest has an eel spear across his knees. Its barbs are close enough to count.*

*The one holding the lantern leans closer.* That's no eel.

It's in our net. *The one with the spear leans out and turns your wrist over with the butt of it, so the light falls on the ink.* Colony.

*Far back across the fen, toward the causeway, a horn blows twice. All three of them look that way. Then they look back at you.*
```

Present at the start: Ellis (the spear), and two of his cousins as a crowd. The horn is the patrol at Grey Water.

## The protagonist's economy

The fen's trade isn't simulated. Eels and peat go out through the landing and grain and iron come in, which is why the landing, Cadfan and Edric exist. What's modelled is the runaway's purse: what it buys and where, how fast it fills, and what keeps it empty. Prices follow the ten-step scale in *Growth, Challenge and Economy*.

**Goods and who sells them:**

| Good | Price | Kind | Does | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Food and a pallet in the Myrtle's loft | free with any coin |  | Rest | The Myrtle |
| Padded jerkin | 1 | Armour | +1 Health | Cadfan's store |
| Moss dressing | 1 | Use up | 2 Health back | Morwen's door |
| Willow draught | 1 | Use up | 2 Strain back | Morwen's door |
| Peat smoke pot | 1, or made | Use up | Alert down 1 | Cadfan's store |
| Fen pole | 1, or made | Help | A boost on chases and crossing the fen | Gethin |
| Gethin and his flatboat | 1 a day | In your pay | Water routes | The dock |
| Gethin's old flatboat | 10 | Open | Water routes, your own | Gethin |
| Eel spear | 10 | Weapon | +2 harm, carried openly | Cadfan's store |
| Mail shirt, patrol sword | taken; Cadfan buys them at 5 and asks nothing | Armour; weapon | +2 Health; +2 harm | The patrol |
| Sergeant Reyne looking the other way | 10 | Bribe | One patrol misses the runaway | Grey Water |
| Low Road passage | 20 |  | Out to Caerwyn | Cadfan |
| The buyout | 100, plus Edric's cut |  | The claim Released | Edric |

Some things aren't for coin: Morwen's work, a Crone's token that opens the family islands, Meyrick's word. They come from the threads.

**Income:**

| Source | Pays | Pace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A shift at the smokehouse by day or the eel traps on run nights | 1 coin a shift, one shift a day | Fed, never ahead |
| Things made from what the fen gives free: smoke pots, poles, moss | 1 coin each from whoever sells that thing: Cadfan for smoke pots, Gethin for poles, Morwen for moss | Slow; better with Focus |
| A job: a barrel run for Cadfan, watching the patrol for Corran, an errand for Morwen | 10 | One or two a week, each with a risk attached |
| Loot: a patrol soldier, Reyne, Orlo | 1, 10, 100 | Fast, and each one brings the colony down on the fen |
| Edric's strongbox, before he pays out on day 11 | 100 and more | Once. It holds what the whole fen is owed for the season |

**The curve:**

- **Day 1:** from nothing to a coin, by a shift or a theft. That's a meal and a pallet.
- **Boat day, day 4:** ten coin from one job buys one thing that changes a fight: a spear, mail, a flatboat or a bribe. In a fight that's worth about a rating step (Growth doc), long before experience pays for one.
- **By day 11:** a big score is possible, and every big score has someone coming after it.
- **The buyout** comes only from Orlo's purse, Edric's strongbox, or every job going for two weeks. The other routes cost no coin and cost something worse.
- **Experience:** the module's 70 to 80 points make a Seasoned character by the end.

**What keeps the purse empty:** no coin means a night in the reeds with whatever hunts there; the patrol closes in every few days; the bribe and the Low Road cost most when coin is shortest; the Crones fine any damage done to a family; and the buyout sits there as the one clean way out.

## Places

Bogwater holds three of the nine; the rest are a short boat ride out. Each description's first sentence stands alone, for a mention or a crowded room. Partitions are listed in the order a visitor reaches them.

### Bogwater

The fen's trading post, on the biggest island near open water: a dock, an alehouse, the Crones' round hall and a few dozen houses on stilts, joined by plank walks that sink a finger's width under every step. It is the parent of the next three places.

### The landing

Bogwater's dock and smokehouse, where the fen sells to the outside, and as far in as most outsiders get. Eel barrels stand three high under reed thatch, and peat smoke from the smokehouse lies flat over the water.

- **The dock:** plank piers on tarred posts, mooring rings, and the Caerwyn boat on boat day.
- **The smokehouse:** a low black shed, eels hanging on rods over peat fires, worked day and night in the run.
- **Cadfan's store:** rope, salt, iron hooks and stoppered jars of Morwen's goods on the shelves, the barrels he packs himself, and his bed behind a curtain.
- *Access and hours:* open to anyone. The store by day, behind the curtain by invitation. The dock is busiest at dawn and on boat day.
- *Things:* the store's door, locked at night (Average to pick).

### The Myrtle

Bogwater's alehouse, named for the bog myrtle in its ale, and the only place in the fen that will sell an outsider a bed. A long room under sagging thatch, benches black with use, a peat fire in a pit in the floor.

- **The taproom:** the benches, the fire pit, Bronwen's barrels, and whoever is drinking.
- **The loft:** straw pallets under the thatch, up a ladder through a trap door that Bronwen bolts from below at closing.
- *Access and hours:* open from midday until the last trap crew comes in. The loft for anyone who has paid.
- *Things:* the trap door, bolted at night (Easy to force, and loud). Edric's strongbox under his pallet on boat nights (Hard).

### The Crones' house

A round hall of tarred alder posts and reed walls, the biggest building in Bogwater, where the Council of Crones sits around a fire nobody lets go out.

- **The hall:** the ring of seats, the fire, and on sitting day the Crones. Swept and empty other days.
- **Meyrick's room:** a side room with a cot, a table, and a stool for whoever has come to bargain.
- **The burning ground:** a scorched stone floor outside, facing the open water, with dry peat and driftwood stacked under a lean-to.
- *Access and hours:* the hall only when summoned or bringing a dispute; Meyrick's room by asking for him; the burning ground open. Meyrick is here most days, the Crones on sitting day.
- *Rules:* nobody here is for sale, and an offer counts against you.

### Nest's island

A salamander family's island of firm ground, ringed with sharpened stakes: a longhouse, smoking racks, and flatboats tied up three deep.

- **The jetty:** the one way in, where visitors stop at the stakes and shout.
- **The longhouse:** three generations of Nest's family, the hearth, and Nest's chair nearest it.
- *Access and hours:* by invitation only. Landing uninvited rouses the island, and killing a trespasser is no crime (Owner). The family is home at dusk; the men are at the traps on run nights.

### Morwen's island

A small island well out from the others: one hut, racks of moss and willow bark drying, and a door with a hatch in it at shoulder height.

- **The door:** where people come for remedies. Morwen talks through the hatch and sells over the sill.
- **The workroom:** pots, a fire, jars on every shelf, and a chest with an iron hasp.
- **The back room:** behind a second door, barred from the workroom side. Something in there scratches at night.
- *Access and hours:* the door by day, the workroom by Morwen's leave, the back room barred.
- *Things:* the poison chest (Average to pick); the back room's bar (Easy to lift from the workroom).

### The eel traps

Wicker fences staked across the channel where it narrows, steering eels into nets and traps, with plank walks along the top. On run nights the crews work them by lantern.

- **The long fence:** Nest's family's, nearest Bogwater.
- **The bend fence:** at the channel's bend, claimed by Nest's family and Tegan's both, and worked by whoever gets there first.
- **The channel:** open water below the fences, the way to Caerwyn, where the patrol stops boats.
- *Access and hours:* crews from dusk on run nights. By day, empty but for whoever is mending.

### The fen

The open fen around Bogwater: reed beds taller than a standing hare, cut by narrow water, old peat diggings flooded into black pools, and wide shallow water where the ducks sit.

- **The reed beds:** where people hide, get lost, or get hunted.
- **The peat diggings:** plank paths between the cuts, where peat is dug and stacked by day.
- **The open water:** shallow and wide, with wildfowlers' hides along the edges.
- *Access:* open. Fog closes the ways to anyone who hasn't learned them, which is Focus building Prepared here.

### Grey Water

A wide grey lake at the fen's edge, where the old causeway to Nospier Point runs out across the water on rotten posts. The patrol camps on the dry end when its rota brings it.

- *Access:* open, and the way out to Port Covenant.

### The Woed's edge

Where the reeds give way to alders standing in black water and the Woed begins. Gatherers meet buyers on a strip of firm ground before the trees, and past it the Hushed's plank bridges go in.

- *Access:* open as far as the trees. Past them, nobody goes alone (Woed doc), and the Woed notices the lost.

**Offstage:** the far islands (Tegan's family and the other Crones), Caerwyn, Port Covenant and Cairn Maw. **In reserve:** the salt pans on the coast, empty in autumn.

## Getting around

Bogwater is the hub. The landing, the Myrtle and the Crones' house are a few minutes apart on the plank walks, and moving between partitions takes no time. Everything else goes by flatboat, on foot, or both.

| From | To | By flatboat | On foot | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bogwater | The fen | 5 min | 10 min | The reed beds start behind the Myrtle |
| Bogwater | The eel traps | 10 min | 20 min | On foot by the plank walk along the channel |
| Bogwater | Nest's island | 20 min | none | Water only |
| Bogwater | Morwen's island | 30 min | 45 min | On foot by a causeway only Morwen keeps up. Fog closes it |
| The fen | Grey Water | none | 1½ hours | By the old causeway. Fog closes it |
| Bogwater | The Woed's edge | 3 hours | 4 hours | Upstream. Fog closes the foot path |
| The eel traps | Caerwyn (exit) | about a day | none | 48 km down the channel |
| Grey Water | Port Covenant (exit) | none | about 5 hours | 22 km on the old causeway |

Why a flatboat matters: without one, Nest's island can't be reached at all, and the Woed's edge is most of a day there and back on a path the fog can shut. Distances are drafts from the Port Covenant module where they exist.

## Threads

Four threads on clocks, each blowing up on its own if the runaway never touches it, and all four landing within a day of sitting day unless the player forces one sooner. The fifth is small: cover and leverage. Day-by-day beats are in the next section.

**1. The ink.** Port Covenant wants its runaway back, and the fen hasn't decided what the runaway is. Bastien Orlo, the Pact's returns officer, comes with the patrol and carries the Arbiters' copy of the contract.

- *What it wants from the runaway:* them gone, one way or the other.
- *Milestones:* still in the fen and not handed over at the end of day 3 (5 XP); through the standoff at the dock (10); the claim Released or Closed (20).
- *If it blows up:* on day 11 Orlo offers a reward at the dock. If the fen hands the runaway over, they go out in chains along the causeway, and the next scene is an escape. If it fights, the landing burns and Port Covenant has its excuse.

**2. The spark.** Corran, envoy from Clan Aurochs, wants the host gathered, and nothing would gather it faster than a colony patrol killing fen folk on fen ground. A runaway the patrol wants is the best bait he's been handed. Ellis and the young men of Nest's family want the patrol bloodied, Nest holds them back, and Meyrick has to answer Corran without promising anything.

- *What it wants from the runaway:* Corran wants them seen on Nest's island; Ellis wants them as his reason.
- *Milestones:* finding out what Corran is doing (5); the war started or stopped (15).
- *If it blows up:* on the night of day 13 the patrol lands on Nest's island, and Ellis's crew is waiting. By sitting day there are bodies to burn, and the Crones vote fighters to the host.

**3. Tamecap.** Morwen's ward, Lowri, is turning. Rabitis takes the afflicted feral, and tamecap from the Woed cures it only early (Owner). Morwen keeps the girl barred in the back room and needs someone sent to the Woed's edge, as witches do (Owner). The fen blames her whenever something goes wrong, and a witch's girl gone feral is the worst thing that can go wrong.

- *What it wants from the runaway:* hands and feet. Morwen can't leave Lowri, and no fen family will run a witch's errand to the Woed. Burning off the ink is what she pays with.
- *Milestones:* tamecap in Morwen's hand by day 8 (10); Lowri's fate settled, cured or hunted (10).
- *If it blows up:* Lowri gets out on the night of day 9. A crew at the traps is hurt, the fen hunts the reeds, and on sitting day the Crones try Morwen. The clock is ours; the disease itself stays open.

**4. The barrels.** Cadfan runs runaways to Caerwyn in eel barrels for the Low Road, which sometimes sells its passengers on (Societies doc). Edric, the great house's buyer, pays the fen less every season because it has nobody else to sell to. The patrol has started stopping boats on the channel.

- *What it wants from the runaway:* Cadfan wants a passenger who can pay. Edric wants eyes on the fen, and doesn't mind whose.
- *Milestones:* a barrel run past the patrol (5); Edric's strongbox, taken or kept safe (5).
- *If it blows up:* on day 11 Orlo has the dock searched. Whatever is behind Cadfan's curtain comes out, and Cadfan is taken or runs.

**5. The bend fence.** Nest's family and Tegan's both claim the bend fence, and it comes before the Crones every autumn. It's a lever on either Crone, and cover for anything done at the traps after dark. *Milestone:* the ruling going the way the runaway wanted (5).

If everything is taken up, the milestones come to 90 XP; most saves will see 70 to 80 with what people give.

## People

Twelve anchors. Each shows their people in one or two traits and a colouring. **Plays like** names a known model for the dynamics only, so motives and pressures don't have to be guessed; names, looks and the details of each life stay original. The four marked Owner keep the Fenmire doc's roles, and Meyrick is the doc's Meurig, spelled the way it's said, which is the owner's call.

The frame borrows two shapes whole: Nest's family under Corran's pressure plays like *The Godfather*, and the runaway against Orlo plays like Valjean and Javert in *Les Misérables*. As a people, the fen folk sit near the Foggy Swamp Tribe from *Avatar: The Last Airbender*: insular swamp folk who let outsiders get lost.

The town as a whole is closest to Morthal in Skyrim, a marsh town that distrusts its wizard. Falion took in an orphan girl, Agni, as his ward and apprentice; the town says he sacrifices children; and he is the only one in the hold who can cure vampirism, if someone fetches what the ritual needs. Morwen and Lowri are built in that shape, with the sickness in the ward. Morthal has one more drama Bogwater hasn't used yet: Falion's sister keeps the town's inn, and nobody knows. Bronwen as Morwen's sister would cost one relationship.

**Dossiers,** in the house-style fields. Corran, Bronwen and Ellis were written first, to set the voice, and the rest follow them.

**Corran**

- *First line:* Clan Aurochs's envoy to the fen, lodging at the Myrtle while he asks who would fight if the host gathered.
- *Looks:* Black-hided and bigger than anyone in Bogwater, a pale stripe down his spine, horns tipped sideways to clear the Myrtle's door. Chalk dust ground into the hair of his forearms.
- *Manner:* Buys the round before anyone asks. Agrees with whoever spoke last, then repeats it at the next table as if they'd sworn to it. Goes still when the colony comes up, and stays still until whoever raised it has finished.
- *Speech:* Slow and warm. Every sentence is a question he knows the answer to. Calls anyone younger than him cousin.

**Bronwen**

- *First line:* Keeps the Myrtle, Bogwater's alehouse and the only place in the fen an outsider can buy a bed.
- *Looks:* A brown hare with one ear notched to the root, sleeves pinned past the elbow, and a ring of keys on a cord that she thumbs while anyone pays.
- *Manner:* Takes coin first and never asks names. Waters the ale for colony accents. Throws out fighters herself, with the poker she keeps in the fire pit.
- *Speech:* Prices, mostly. Answers a question with what it costs.

**Ellis**

- *First line:* Nest's grandson, who runs his family's crew at the long fence and wants the colony patrol dead.
- *Looks:* Lean and glossy black, orange blotches down his back, and an eel spear he carries everywhere, its barbs wrapped in rag indoors.
- *Manner:* Laughs first and loudest. Picks fights with anyone older and drops them the moment Nest says his name. Counts the patrol's men out loud every time he sees them.
- *Speech:* Fast, and too loud for the room. Talks about the patrol in the past tense.

**Nest**

- *First line:* The eldest of the Council of Crones, who speaks last at every sitting and remembers which family wronged which three generations back.
- *Looks:* Her skin has gone dull and dry with age, the yellow spots faded nearly to grey, and she walks with a boat pole she uses as a staff.
- *Manner:* Lets everyone else speak first, then rules. Never raises her voice; lowers it, so the room has to lean in. Keeps every grudge in order and trades one away when the price is right.
- *Speech:* Slow and quiet. Answers a question with a family's name and a year.

**Morwen**

- *First line:* The fen's witch, who sells remedies through a hatch in her door and lives alone on her own island with a girl someone left at Bogwater's edge.
- *Looks:* Lean and dun, fingers stained brown to the second knuckle, and she smells of peat smoke and bog myrtle.
- *Manner:* Sells first and asks nothing. These days she bars her door by noon and answers it with a knife in her other hand.
- *Speech:* Doses and prices, in that order. A question gets the price again.

**Cadfan**

- *First line:* A fen trader who runs the store and smokehouse at Bogwater's landing, and takes the fen's barrels down the channel to Caerwyn every week.
- *Looks:* Broad and heavy-shouldered, the white of his face stripe gone yellow at the edges.
- *Manner:* Easier with outsiders than fen folk ought to be, and the islands don't trust him for it. Counts the barrels twice and the coin once. Gets runaways downriver for a price, and doesn't say what happens at the other end.
- *Speech:* Friendly and round, all cousin and friend, and never a straight number.

**Meyrick**

- *First line:* The bog-keeper, the man the Crones chose to speak for the fen to outsiders, and the only fen voice the colony will listen to.
- *Looks:* Big and grey-brown, with long hare's ears and a jackalope's antlers, one tine snapped off.
- *Manner:* Says little and promises less, which suits the Crones. Outsiders find him reasonable; at home that counts against him. Stands while others sit, until Nest tells him otherwise.
- *Speech:* Slow, and never a promise. Repeats your offer back to you so you hear how it sounds.

**Lowri**

- *First line:* Morwen's ward, a girl of eleven left at Bogwater's edge as a baby and raised in the witch's craft.
- *Looks:* Small and sandy, a white blaze down her face, fingers already stained from Morwen's pots.
- *Manner:* Quick, curious and fond of mud. Lately she doesn't answer to her name, and sleeps through the day.
- *Speech:* Used to talk through every chore. Now she hums one note, over and over.

**Tegan**

- *First line:* A Crone of the Council and Cadfan's mother, head of a badger family on the far islands that has fought Nest's family over the bend fence for three generations.
- *Looks:* Heavy and grizzled, her face stripes nearly gone, with a necklace of fish hooks that clinks when she walks.
- *Manner:* Blunt and loud in a hall that prizes quiet. Votes against Nest on principle and against outsiders on instinct. Asks after Cadfan's health in the same breath as his prices.
- *Speech:* Loud, short, and full of fish-market insults.

**Gethin**

- *First line:* Bronwen's son, who poles a flatboat for fares from Bogwater's dock and takes anyone anywhere if they pay first.
- *Looks:* A long-legged brown hare with brass rings in both ears and a boat pole he never puts down.
- *Manner:* Cheerful, cheap and loose-mouthed after two drinks. Takes the fare first, then asks where.
- *Speech:* Talks over his shoulder while he poles, mostly about other people's business.

**Edric**

- *First line:* A great house's eel buyer from Caerwyn, who comes on the boat every Monday and pays the fen less than last year.
- *Looks:* Rust-red with black legs, a Caerwyn coat with horn buttons, and a strongbox chained to his left wrist.
- *Manner:* Pleasant, punctual and never in a hurry to pay. Remembers every trader's debts and none of their names.
- *Speech:* City-smooth, full of regret about prices he set himself.

**Bastien Orlo**

- *First line:* The Pact's returns officer, who brings runaways back to their contracts and carries the Arbiters' copy of the runaway's indenture.
- *Looks:* Tall and clean-shaven, gloves on in all weather, an oilcloth satchel chained to his belt.
- *Manner:* Waits as long as it takes. Follows the contract to the letter, and takes no money, no drink and no excuses. Keeps a tally of every runaway he has brought back, and reads it aloud to the next one.
- *Speech:* Formal, exact and quiet. Quotes the contract clause by clause.

**Mattias Reyne**

- *First line:* The patrol sergeant out of Port Covenant, who works the fen border for the garrison and works any deal on the side.
- *Looks:* A sergeant's sash over a coat gone green at the seams, and a cough he doesn't cover.
- *Manner:* Lazy where nobody's watching and brisk where somebody is. Takes coin to look the other way.
- *Speech:* Cheerful soldier's cant between coughs. Calls everyone friend, prisoners included.

**The patrol**

- *First line:* Two garrison soldiers on Reyne's patrol, wet through and sick of the fen.
- *Looks:* Two humans in oiled capes and padded coats, with spears, one young and one not.
- *Manner:* Do what Reyne says and nothing more, unless the fen gives them a reason.
- *Speech:* Grumble to each other in colony slang.

**Ellis's cousins**

- *First line:* Ellis's two cousins, who work the long fence with him and go where he goes.
- *Looks:* Two young salamanders, black with yellow blotches, mud to the elbows.
- *Manner:* Laugh when Ellis laughs. Fight when Ellis fights.
- *Speech:* Finish each other's jokes, badly.

**The Hushed traders**

- *First line:* Two of the Hushed, who come to the Woed's edge in the mornings to sell what the wood gives, tamecap included.
- *Looks:* Two figures in undyed wool, hoods up, never more than an arm's length apart.
- *Manner:* Sell, and say nothing else to outsiders. Won't deal with fen folk at all.
- *Speech:* Only one of them talks, and only in a whisper.

## The body

Last month the patrol stopped Cadfan's boat on the channel. Before they came aboard, Cadfan rolled two heavy barrels over the side, and one of them had a Low Road passenger in it: a human man, about the runaway's size. Cadfan pulled him out after dark and put him under the barrels at the back of the smokehouse, wrapped in sacking. Nobody has noticed. The smokehouse smells of everything.

Cadfan can't sink him: the fen burns its dead so no soul is trapped, and Cadfan is fen enough to believe it. He can't burn him either, because the Crones would ask how an outsider came to be in his barrels.

That's what makes the drowned man worth having. Burned under the runaway's name, he closes the Pact's claim, sends the patrol home and lets his own soul go. Meyrick is the one who can put that to the Crones and send word to Grey Water, so he has to be won first. If Orlo ever sees the runaway alive afterwards, the claim is Owed again; that part is the narrator's for now.

## The Woed

The Woed's edge is as far as the module goes. The Hushed traders stand on the strip of firm ground before the trees in the mornings, sell tamecap to the runaway and nothing to fen folk, and go back in by their plank bridges at noon. No route goes past them.

What the Woed does stays with the narrator, and stays small. It notices the lost and the hopeless, and a runaway with nothing is both. One who waits at the edge past noon hears the birds stop, and finds a path behind them that wasn't there in the morning, going in. Nothing attacks. The Hushed won't take anyone with them, and won't say why.

## Where it stands

Built in [PR #22](https://github.com/HowlingWhispers/HW-Praxis/pull/22) as Praxis 0.20.0: the module file, with 17 people, 38 places and 16 scene actions, and nine tests. One of them runs forty days of schedules and fails if any place holds more than four people at once. The engine learned two things for it: a schedule rule can wait on a state, so a thread's outcome moves people, and a scene action can set a world state.

- [x] The frame: places and partitions, routes, the ink as two states, the purse, the threads, the opening.
- [x] Time: the fen's calendar, seasons and weather, one daily round per person.
- [x] Dossiers for everyone, households, the twelve pairs, and the numbers with their reasons.
- [x] World states for the threads' outcomes, wired into schedules and scene actions.
- [x] The body, and how far the Woed goes.
- [ ] Partitions in the engine. Rooms are child places with free hops for now, which plays the same.
- [ ] Calendar events. Sitting day and boat day are each person's own schedule rule; one event record would carry everyone.
- [ ] Weather that shuts routes. Fog and floods are in the descriptions only.
- [ ] Milestones without a scene action: still free at the end of day 3, the dock standoff, the bend fence ruling. Orlo putting the claim back to Owed when he sees the runaway alive.
- [ ] Rumours: one line per thread that the taproom passes on.
- [ ] Retire the Port Covenant test module once Bogwater's tests cover what its tests do.

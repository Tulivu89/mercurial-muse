## How power works on the coast

The city's money reaches nearly every other power, through loans, trade and tithes, while its titled ruler commands nothing. *(Suggestion)*

&#91;embedded content: Landfall Coast · power map\]

The city's crime families stand with the great houses against the pirates, because piracy hurts the trade they live off. The jarldoms are tied to the city through the dock families, by blood.

## Governance by location

Most places on the coast are run by someone other than whoever holds the title. *(Suggestion)*

| Location | Governing faction | In charge | How power actually works |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| City-state | The merchant council | The Harbour Lord, in name | The title passes down an old first-peoples line and commands no army. The council's great houses control customs and the harbour chain, the dock families control who works, and the Harbour Lord signs what the council writes |
| Jarnmark (north) | The jarl's hall and the thing | The jarl of Jarnmark | Free wolves acclaim a jarl at the thing, an open assembly. Far from Caerwyn and its trade, Jarnmark lives by fishing, raiding and the old ways, and is poorer for it. Its quiet tie to the pirates is its leverage over Suthmark |
| Suthmark (south) | The jarl's hall and the thing | The jarl of Suthmark | Same customs, but closer to Caerwyn. It collects sea tolls, works thralls from Thrallstrand and trades with the great houses, and looks down on Jarnmark |
| The Rookery, on the Crowns | The captains' articles | The Pirate Queen | A loose confederation. Each crew answers to its own captain and articles; the pirate crown only settles disputes between crews docked at the Rookery and commands its defence. Crews help each other only when all of them are in trouble |
| Port Covenant | The Pact, under the Covenant | The Governor and the High Arbiter | Nothing binds anyone until they sign it, and no one enters the port without signing. The Arbiters, a monastic order of notaries, keep the contracts. See the Port Covenant doc |
| Wreck coast camps | Suthmark's overseers | The thrall overseer | Thralls have no rights. Inside the camps, refugees answer to their own elder, whom the overseers use and the thralls trust |
| Fenmire | The Council of Crones | The oldest family matriarchs; the bog-keeper speaks for them outside the fen | Most fen folk live in private, defensive family clusters. The Crones settle every matter at Bogwater and choose a man, the bog-keeper, to represent the fen to other groups. See the Fenmire doc |
| Barrow downs | Barrow-keeper clans | Each clan's head, who wins it by challenge, and a war leader in wartime | Each clan keeps its own barrows, and the clans meet to choose a war leader when one is needed. With no war imminent, none has been chosen. See the Cairn Maw doc, which takes precedence |
| River-valley farms | Landholders and reeves | Each valley's leading landholder | They hold their own land and pay tithes to whoever is strongest that year, which is mostly the city |
| The mudflats (city district) | No one official | Whoever can hold it | The council neither taxes nor polices the mudflats. A refugee elder and the dock families compete to run them |

## Factions that cut across places

Six factions belong to no single location, and they carry most of the coast's money and violence from one place to another. *(Suggestion)*

| Faction | Base | Members | Wants | Reach |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The great houses | The city's counting row | Foxfolk merchant families, wolf shipping houses, a few boarfolk grain houses, and the memory of a ferret shipping house that fell | A grip on sea and river trade | Lend to the farms, and keep the dock families close |
| The dock families | The wolf docks | Wolf-descended dock clans | Control of dock labour and of everything smuggled through it | Tied to the jarldoms by blood and to the great houses by business. Pirates cut into the trade they skim |
| The salvage syndicate | The edge of the drowned ward | Otterfolk divers and their backers | The sole right to work the drowned ward | Sells salvage licences, and buys from anyone who comes out alive |
| The underground | The mudflats and the wreck coast | Escaped thralls, refugees, sympathetic smugglers | Thralls out of the jarldoms | Charges what refugees can pay, and sometimes sells them on |
| The barrow host | The barrow downs | First-peoples fighters from the downs and fens | Their land back, from the colony, the jarldoms and anyone else holding it | Raids colony surveyors and jarl tax parties |
| Root-road pilots | The skerries | A handful of guides, mostly pirates | Their routes kept secret | Every smuggler who uses the roads needs one, and pilots die young |

## The Hushed

The Hushed are the single communal cult that lives with the Woed, founded generations ago by a serpent who came out of the wood claiming to have communed with the whispering dead. Their full lore is in [Abernys — The Woed](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/a42ca5b5-3414-47de-aecb-b7b3b786eed9), which takes precedence over anything earlier. *(Owner)*

- **Leader:** the Paterfamilias, father to all, a title passed from father to son.
- **Life:** communal. Nobody is alone, everything is shared, leaders audit the flock, and everyone is expected to share their feelings about each other and themselves.
- **Work:** they maintain the plank bridges, hire out as guides without sharing their maps, and harvest bog iron, black bog oak, deep-wood plants and the mushroom that cures Rabitis.
- **Outsiders:** trade and guiding only. Members don't socialize outside.

Relationships *(Suggestion)*:

- **With the jarldoms:** their best customers for bog iron. The jarls would take the wood themselves if they could survive it.
- **With the barrow host:** the Hushed stay out of the war to retake the land, which the host resents.
- **With the salvage syndicate:** the syndicate wants to hire Hushed guides for the drowned ward, where the wood's roots run.

## Law by jurisdiction

Each jurisdiction treats owning people and predation differently, so anyone crossing between them crosses into a different law. *(Suggestion)*

| Jurisdiction | Owning people | Predation | Enforced by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| City-state | Legal with council-sealed papers. The council doesn't chase runaways into the mudflats | A blood price set by witnesses and money | The council's watch, paid by the great houses |
| Jarldoms | Thralldom is law. Whether thralls' children are born free is disputed | Judged at the thing. A wolf who kills prey pays the victim's kin; killing a thrall only costs the owner | The jarl's hall and the lawspeaker |
| The Rookery | Slaves are cargo | Settled by the articles: a crewmate's killer is marooned or worse | The captains, by vote |
| Port Covenant | Indenture by signed contract, for any people, children included. Not counted as slavery, since every party agreed | Whatever the contracts a party has signed say. Law binds only those who sign it | The governor's court, the garrison and the Arbiters |
| Fenmire | Despised and forbidden. Slavers caught in the fen don't leave | Exile or death, whatever the cause. Killing a trespasser on a family cluster is no crime | The Council of Crones |
| Barrow downs | Forbidden. Slavers caught on the downs don't leave | Exile or death for any predation, whatever the cause | Each clan's head |
| River-valley farms | Bonded labour for debt | Whatever the strongest tithe-taker enforces that year | Landholders and their reeves |

## The fen folk

Even in the first peoples' own days, the fens were never a pleasant or easy place to live, and the other first peoples still look down on the people who stayed. The fen folk are proud of the life they eke out anyway. *(Owner)*

The fen folk are harefolk, badgerfolk and salamanders. *(Owner)* Salamanders are new to the setting; none appear among Bitterroot's peoples.

- **Independent.** They answer to their own families and their own Crones, and to no one beyond the fen.
- **Against slavery.** They despise it.
- **Long memories.** They still hold grudges against the wolves and against every people that came after.
- **Wary.** They can be particularly distrustful of outsiders.

To the rest of Abernys they are backward bog folk. To themselves they are the ones who never needed anybody.

### The dead

The fen folk don't bury their dead. They burn them, so there is no risk of a soul being trapped. That sets them firmly against the Hushed, who call dying in the Woed an honor. *(Owner)*

## Land, water and ordinary danger

Fenmire is low, waterlogged ground draining off the Woed toward the south coast *(Region seed)*. The details below are a starting shape. *(Suggestion)*

| Ground | What it's like |
| --- | --- |
| Reed beds | The open fen: reeds taller than a standing hare, cut by narrow channels |
| Meres | Shallow lakes left where the peat was cut out generations ago |
| Peat bog | The higher, spongier ground nearer the Woed, where peat is cut |
| Hummocks | Islands of firm ground above the wet, where families build |
| Salt marsh | Where the fen meets the coast and the tide comes in |
| Causeways | Raised paths between hummocks, known to locals and easy for strangers to lose *(Societies doc)* |

Punts and flat-bottomed boats carry most travel and trade. Ordinary danger is enough to kill a stranger: winter flooding, marsh fever, sucking mud, fog, and causeways that end in open water.

## Bogwater and the family clusters

### Bogwater

Bogwater is the closest thing Fenmire has to a town: the fen's trading post and the place where disputes between families are settled. Outsiders are viewed with suspicion but allowed to trade there, and fen traders make the regular trek between Bogwater and the other regions. *(Owner)*

Possible features *(Suggestion)*:

- **The landing**, where Caerwyn's boats tie up and fen goods change hands. It's as far into the fen as most outsiders get.
- **The Crones' house**, where the Council of Crones sits and families bring their disputes.
- **Trade stores**: smoked eels, cut peat, reed bundles and witch-goods waiting for buyers.

### The family clusters

Most fen folk live in clusters of families, not in Bogwater. The clusters are very private and defensive. Any incursion is likely to be treated as a threat and met with violence, and killing a trespasser is no crime. *(Owner)* Each cluster could hold its own hummock, with its own peat cuttings, eel traps and reed beds *(Suggestion)*. Named families come in a later pass across all communities.

In Orbis, each cluster is a family record, and Bogwater is a village record whose description calls it a trading post.

## Witchcraft

Fenmire has strong traditions of witchcraft. It is ritual and alchemical work rather than spell-casting, and nothing about it is confirmed supernatural. *(Owner)*

Witchcraft can be used to help or to harm, and both the craft and the witches are treated with suspicion. Even the fen folk keep a healthy wariness while they deal with them. *(Owner)*

### The witches *(Owner)*

- The craft passes down mostly through women who stay unmarried and live alone.
- Witches sometimes take in abandoned girls and raise them in the practice.
- They have their own network and their own moot, and little is known of either.

### What the witches claim *(Belief)*

- Their power comes from their connection with *their* land, the fen.
- The Woed is not of Abernys. It is an outlet from something else, something other.

The Woed is still the one place they draw on outside the fen: when a witch needs a Woed ingredient, she sends someone to procure it. *(Owner)*

### What witches make *(Suggestion)*

Each of these draws on a real fen plant or practice, so much of it works for ordinary reasons. Whether any of it works for more than that is left open.

| Work | Made from | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wound dressings | Sphagnum moss, dried and packed | Soaking up blood and keeping wounds clean |
| Fever draughts | Willow bark | Fevers and pain |
| Leech-craft | Live leeches | Bloodletting and bruises |
| Biting-fly oil | Bog myrtle | Keeping off midges; also flavours ale |
| Poisons | Water hemlock and other fen plants | Rats, and anything else the buyer doesn't say |
| Peat-smoking | Slow peat fires | Preserving eels and meat through winter |

### Reputation *(Belief)*

Caerwyn buys fen remedies and calls the people who make them hags. Other first peoples count the witchcraft as more proof of how backward the fen is, and send for a fen witch anyway when a fever won't break.

The rituals themselves and the witches' moot stay open, and little known by design.

## Economy

Fenmire's economy should be distinct from the Woed's and get first claim on fen resources. Its trade relies most on Caerwyn. *(Owner)* The goods below are a proposed shape. *(Suggestion unless marked)*

| Good | What it is | Main buyer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eels | Trapped, smoked over peat and packed in barrels. The fen's staple and its best-known export *(Region seed)* | Caerwyn |
| Peat | Cut, stacked and dried as fuel *(Region seed)* | Caerwyn hearths, and the fen's own |
| Reeds | Thatch, baskets, mats and rushlights *(Region seed)* | Caerwyn builders |
| Sphagnum moss | Wound dressing | Caerwyn healers |
| Witch-goods | Remedies, oils, preserves and poisons | Caerwyn, quietly, and other first peoples |
| Wildfowl and eggs | Caught in the reed beds | Bogwater and Caerwyn markets |
| Salt | Boiled from the coastal salt marsh | Caerwyn, and the fen's own eel-curing |

The grudges shape the trade: fen goods go to Caerwyn and to other first peoples, and not to the jarldoms or Port Covenant if the fen folk can help it.

### Splitting resources with the Woed

| Fenmire | The Woed |
| --- | --- |
| Eels, peat, reeds, sphagnum moss, wildfowl, salt, witch-goods | Bog iron, black bog oak, tamecap, deep-wood plants |

Agreed: sphagnum moss moves from the Woed to Fenmire, and the Woed keeps what only a haunted wood yields. *(Owner)* The two don't trade with each other; a witch's errand for a Woed ingredient is the exception.

## Key people

| Meurig | Jackalope, a rare hare and cervid hybrid | The bog-keeper, chosen by the Crones to speak for the fen | To keep the Crones' trust | The only voice the fen gives outsiders | Has to face Port Covenant after every clash, and the barrow host wants fen fighters |

### Nest

Her skin has gone dull and dry with age, the yellow spots faded nearly to grey, and she walks with a punt pole she uses as a staff. At the Council she speaks last. She remembers which family wronged which three generations back, and rules with that in mind.

### Morwen

Lean and quiet, fingers stained brown to the second knuckle, and she smells of peat smoke and bog myrtle. She lives alone on her own hummock and is raising a girl someone left at the edge of Bogwater. People who come to her for a remedy don't stay to talk.

### Cadfan

A weathered badger with a punt and a Caerwyn buyer's mark on his barrels. He's easier with outsiders than most fen folk, which is why he's useful and why the clusters don't fully trust him.

### Meurig

A big, slow-spoken jackalope with long hare's ears and one antler tine snapped off. He says little and promises less, which suits the Crones. Outsiders find him reasonable. At home that counts against him.

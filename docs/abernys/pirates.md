## The Crowns

A crescent of rock islets off Caerwyn, named by sailors for its tiara shape. Its curve shelters a lagoon with one narrow mouth, which is why pirates can hold it. *(Owner's name and shape; details suggested)*

| Why pirates hold it | Too rocky for large warships, close to the richest shipping lanes, and defended from a single mouth |

## The Rookery

The pirates' den on the largest of the Crowns: black markets, brothels and taverns, with slavery in every form from pressed crew to chattel. *(Owner)* The name fits twice: rooks nest crowded on cliffs, and "rookery" was old slang for a slum. *(Suggestion)*

### Quarters *(Suggestion)*

| Quarter | What's there |
| --- | --- |
| The moorings | Crews tie up inside the lagoon. The crown assigns berths, and good ones are fought over |
| The market | Stolen cargo sold in the open to buyers who won't admit at home that they came |
| The pens | Captives held for ransom or sale |
| The stair | Taverns and brothels packed along the cliff stair between the moorings and the market, where crews spend their shares and news changes hands |
| The cisterns | Cut into the rock and guarded by the crown's own people |
| The crown's hold | A walled cave on the southern horn, overlooking the lagoon mouth, with the boom winch and the Rookery's defence |
| The deep caves | Where root-road pilots and smugglers come and go |

### Slavery

The Rookery deals in every kind of unfree person. *(Owner; categories suggested)*

- **Pressed crew** are taken from captured ships and made to serve. They earn a share and can win full standing, as the Pirate Queen did.
- **Ransom captives** are wealthy passengers held until their families or houses pay.
- **Debt slaves** are pirates and dock hands worked until what they owe is paid.
- **Chattel** are sold outright in the pens, many to Suthmark as thralls.

## The pirate confederation

The pirates of Abernys are a loose confederation of crews, not a fleet, and they only help each other when all of them are in trouble. *(Owner)*

### Structure

- **Crews.** Each crew elects its captain and quartermaster and signs its own articles, which set shares, punishments and how a captain is voted out. Real pirate crews worked this way. *(Suggestion)*
- **The crown.** Whoever holds it is called queen or king, but the title is weaker than it sounds. The holder settles matters between crews while they're docked at the Rookery and oversees its defence. *(Owner)* The captains in port choose the holder by vote, and the title lasts only while they back it and the Rookery stands. *(Suggestion)*
- **When crews unite.** A hunter fleet, a blockade or an attack on the Crowns. The rest of the time they compete for the same prizes.

### The Rookery peace *(Suggestion)*

Four rules hold in port, enforced by the crown:

- No blood spilled between crews; quarrels go to the crown.
- Every sale in the market pays the crown a share, which keeps the defence manned and the cisterns guarded.
- A crew that leads hunters to the Crowns pays for the damage.
- In dry seasons the crown rations the water.

### Who sails *(Suggestion)*

Ferrets, rats, otters, stoats and weasels make up most crews, with wolves as muscle, including younger sons out of the jarldoms. Escaped thralls from Thrallstrand and pressed humans sail too, along with a few of every other people. Each crew's articles settle predation among its own; in port, the crown does.

## Ships, crews and trade

Pirate ships are small, fast and shallow enough to cross reefs where hunters can't follow; large captured merchantmen are stripped and sold rather than sailed. Human-built hulls from Port Covenant are the prize every captain wants. *(Suggestion)*

| Source of income | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| Prizes | Cargo taken at sea, mostly from great-house ships out of Caerwyn, and sold in the Rookery market |
| Ransom | Captured passengers held until their families or houses pay |
| Slaving | Captives sold in the pens, many to Suthmark |
| Smuggling | Goods moved past Caerwyn's customs and, rarely, through the root roads |
| Protection | Fishing villages and small traders pay not to be raided |

Most stolen cargo ends up with buyers in the jarldoms and with Port Covenant, which is short of other trading partners and buys from the pirates more than most. *(Suggestion)*

## The Pirate Queen

A ferret who grew up a merchant's daughter and now holds the pirate crown, sailing for revenge on the great houses more than for plunder. Her name is still open; options are in the names doc. *(Owner)*

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| People | Ferret *(Owner)* |
| Born | A daughter of one of Caerwyn's great houses *(Owner)* |
| Role | Holds the pirate crown: settles disputes between crews in port and oversees the Rookery's defence *(Owner)*. Also captains her own ship *(Suggestion)* |
| Drive | Revenge on the great houses *(Owner)* |

### Background *(Owner)*

Her father owned the largest merchant vessel sailing out of Caerwyn, and she practically grew up aboard it. Business dealings and treachery brought her house down. She watched her father taken to debtors' prison; her mother took her own life; she was left on the streets.

It was a rough existence, but once she was old enough she found work on ships. When her ship was captured, she was pressed into the pirates' crew. That was where she flourished, and in time she rose to command.

### What she wants *(Owner)*

Her mission is revenge against the great houses more than plunder. That often puts her at odds with other captains, but it also gives her a single drive, where pirates who sail for themselves eventually fail. She wants all pirates to succeed together, so she has a greater force with which to hurt her enemies.

### How she works *(Suggestion)*

- She reads a manifest and a ledger better than most captains, because she was raised in a merchant house and on its ship. She picks targets by who owns the hull.
- She pays her crew fully and on time, which buys a loyalty that plunder-minded captains can't match.
- She offers common sailors on captured ships a place in her crew, the same choice she was once given, and saves her cruelty for house officers.

### Pressure *(Suggestion)*

Her war draws hunters to the Crowns, and captains who only want prizes resent paying for her revenge. Holding the crown depends on proving that her war makes them richer, not poorer.

## Pirates and the other powers

Every power on the coast either hunts the pirates, buys from them, or both. *(Suggestion unless marked)*

| Power | Stance | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Caerwyn's great houses | Enemies | Pirates take their ships, and the Queen picks her targets by house. The houses hire hunter ships to chase crews |
| Caerwyn's crime families | Enemies | Piracy hurts the trade they skim, so they back the hangings *(Owner)* |
| Jarnmark | Quiet ally | The jarl's younger son captains a crew. Nobody says it aloud, but pirates spare Jarnmark's boats and hit Suthmark's trade, which gives poorer Jarnmark leverage over its richer neighbour |
| Suthmark | Customer and prey | Buys thralls from the pens, while pirates take its toll ships and trade |
| Port Covenant | Steady buyers | Short of other options, the Pact trades with the pirates more than most, and Caerwyn takes the worse of the raiding |
| The barrow host | Occasional customers | Buys weapons the pirates took from colony and city ships |
| Root-road pilots | Part of the confederation | Most pilots are pirates |

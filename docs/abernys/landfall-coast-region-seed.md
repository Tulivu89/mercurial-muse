## What's agreed

These come straight from the conversation so far.

- Same universe as Bitterroot. Bitterroot itself stays as written.
- Dark fantasy.
- An island, possibly with smaller islands around it, a month or two's sail from Bitterroot. Nobody has dared that crossing yet.
- One coastal region first, expanded as the lore and story allow.
- Travel is mostly overland, with seafaring as an option.
- A coastal city-state, bigger and busier than Hollowmere, with a figurehead ruler under the real power of merchants and organized crime.
- A pirate den.
- Layered migrations: a first people with no memory of arriving, later waves that migrated, conquered and mixed, and humans as the latest.
- Wolves are the island's sea raiders turned rulers of a region or two, and are still heavily present among pirates.
- Pirates are mostly rodents and mustelids — ferrets, rats, otters — with wolf muscle.
- Humans arrived a few generations ago at different places with different outcomes: colonizers in one, refugees in another. Mixed families and established feuds already exist.
- Old magic: woods like the Whispering Woods, connected to them through extremely deep roots; root caves and passages under the ocean, a dangerous way across; a drowned, haunted part of the city that scavengers enter and often don't leave; haunted bogs.

## Migration layers

The first peoples are prey peoples, and both conquests that followed were by predators, so the predator–prey conflict is also the island's history of occupation. Humans fit neither side, which makes them the strangest thing on the island. *(Suggestion)*

| Wave | Peoples | How they came | Where they are now | Standing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First peoples | Harefolk, badgerfolk, salamanders, cervidfolk, caprinefolk, bovinefolk | No memory of arriving | Barrow downs, fen villages, the city's old quarter | Conquered twice and still the most numerous |
| Earlier migration | Foxfolk, boarfolk, bearfolk (Bitterroot peoples) | Legend only; the route is lost | River-valley farms, the city's merchant houses | Mixed in with the first peoples; settled, prosperous, distrusted by both sides |
| Sea raiders | Wolves | Longships from the north | Fjord jarldoms, the city's wolf docks, pirate crews | Rulers of the north coast |
| Pirates | Ferrets, rats, otters, stoats, weasels | Island-born, escaped or outcast | The Rookery on the Crowns, and Caerwyn's underworld | Outside every law but useful to most |
| Humans | Colonizers and refugees | A few generations ago, on ships better than anyone else's | The southern colony, thralls on the wreck coast, the city's mudflats | Rulers in one place, property in another |

The two human landings share a cause: a single disaster in the homeland sent out both the colonists and the refugees. The disaster stays unwritten. *(Owner)*

**The Wailing.** A plague struck every species on Abernys before or around the humans' arrival, beginning at Nospier Point. Humans were immune, and settled the half-empty city while everyone else kept away. Details are in the Port Covenant doc. *(Owner)*

## The old magic

The island's old magic can work as one system: a wood kin to the Whispering Woods, whose roots reach into the drowned ward and under the sea. *(Suggestion)*

- **The bog-wood.** A low, wet forest of alder and oak standing in peat, joined to the Whispering Woods through roots too deep to trace. Its feel can differ from the Woods; how it behaves is left open. One community lives with it: the Hushed, a cult covered in the Woed doc, which now takes precedence.
- **The drowned ward.** A storm surge drove up the estuary and drowned the city's low south bank. The ruins sit in the tide. Scavengers go in at low water for what was abandoned and have to be out before the tide turns, and many don't come back. Option: the bog-wood's roots have grown down the riverbed and through the ruins, which is why the ward is haunted rather than merely dangerous.
- **The root roads.** Caves where the great roots run, with passages under the sea. They open in the deepest sea caves of the Crowns and somewhere inside the bog-wood. A crossing is possible, but the networks are easy to get lost in and few survive. Option: the pirates know a few short stretches and use them for smuggling, and lose crews doing it.
- **The haunted bogs.** The Woed's peat preserves its dead, always near the surface in the same position of rest. The fen folk burn their own dead so no soul can be trapped. See the Woed and Fenmire docs, which take precedence.

One question to keep open: did the earlier migration from Bitterroot come through the root roads instead of by sea? That would explain why nobody remembers a route.

## Communities

Eight communities cover the coast, each with a livelihood that ties it to the others and a dark hook to build stories on. *(Suggestion)*

| Community | Where | Peoples | Lives by | Dark hook |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Caerwyn | Estuary mouth | Every people | Sea and river trade, salvage, vice | Crime families sell salvage rights to the drowned ward |
| Jarnmark and Suthmark | Fjord coast | Wolves, with thralls | Sea tolls, fishing, raiding | Human refugees work their halls as thralls; younger sons sail with the pirates |
| The Rookery | The Crowns, offshore | Ferrets, rats, otters, stoats, weasels; wolf muscle | Plunder, fencing, slaving, smuggling | They use the shallowest root roads and lose crews in them |
| Port Covenant | Southern headland | Humans, and beastkin signed into the Pact | Shipbuilding, fishing, trade | Nothing binds anyone until they sign, no one enters without signing, and most of the poor are indentured |
| Thrallstrand camps | Below the jarldoms | Humans | Forced labour | Escapees head south for the city, and smugglers charge as much as the jarls would |
| Fenmire villages | Edge of the bogs | Harefolk, badgerfolk, salamanders | Eels, peat, reeds, sphagnum moss, witch-goods | Private family clusters kill trespassers, and clashes with Port Covenant patrols are common |
| Cairn Maw | Chalk uplands | Bovinefolk, cervidfolk, caprinefolk (rams and goats) | Flint, lime, honey, keeping the barrows | Colonists want the grave goods, and the keepers kill diggers |
| River-valley farms | Upriver floodplain | Foxfolk, boarfolk, bearfolk, mixed with first peoples | Grain and cattle for the city | They pay tithes to whoever is strongest that year, and keep a legend of a land across the sea |

The city's districts can follow the same waves: an old quay for the first peoples, the wolf docks, a counting row of foxfolk merchant houses, the mudflats where refugees and escaped thralls live, and the drowned ward.

## Tensions

Every power on the coast is in at least one live conflict, and most of them trace back to a migration. *(Suggestion)*

- **First peoples and the jarldoms:** an old conquest that never fully settled, over land the first peoples mean to take back.
- **First peoples and the colony:** border clashes on the downs.
- **Jarldoms and the human refugees:** thralldom, plus the escapees the city shelters as cheap labour.
- **The city and the pirates:** the city hangs pirates, and its crime families back the hangings, because piracy hurts the trade they live off.
- **The colony and the city:** two ports competing for the same trade.
- **Humans and everyone:** they are neither predator nor prey, so nobody knows how to read them.

# HOG Wild: the framework

Nov 15, 2026 · @AmbiProp · The framework for HOG Wild, built over five checkpoints (`plan.md`) and now complete. This file holds the show, the house, the cast, the seeds, the grounding and the ties. Checkpoint 4 is `01-decisions.md`, checkpoint 5 is `02-frame.md`, the looks are `looks.md`, and Fable's brief is `handoff.md`. Nothing here is prose a character would say. Fishbowl, Halcyon, Overclock and the Mile are still placeholder names.

## 1. The show (settled)

**The vibe.** HOG Wild is a glass-fronted house on the city's brightest street. Beautiful people live loud in it, and the whole city watches them on a screen six storeys tall above the door. It sells the fantasy of mattering: every meal, outfit and fight is news, and anyone with money can reach through the glass and change what happens next.

**The house.** Three floors of a former car showroom on the Mile, the city's shopping strip, with a basement and a roof. The street side is glass from the pavement to the roof. The screen above the door plays the feed back to the street with the chat scrolling down one side, and there are nearly always fans on the pavement.

**Who owns it, and who pays.**
- **Fishbowl** owns the house and the feed, and manages the talent. It holds the residents' contracts and takes a cut of every bid. It earns from ads, subscriptions and bids.
- **Sponsors** pay to be in shot: the furniture, the clothes, the drinks in the fridge, and the residents' augmentations.
- **Halcyon**, Nova's maker, pays Fishbowl for her place in the house. Nova is a product launch, so a season on camera is her advert, and whatever the audience asks to change in her is Halcyon's market research.

**What the cameras see.** Everything, always. It's always live, and cameras can be anywhere.
- **Subscriptions are tiered.** The bedrooms and the showers are behind the higher tiers.
- **The top tier gets a resident's eyes.** A resident with an eye-implant camera streams what she sees.
- **The Cold Room is the one room without cameras.** Nova's hardware is in it, and Halcyon's contract bans filming it. It's dark because of a contract. Nobody's privacy came into it.

**What the audience can buy.** Bids, which the highest bidder wins and a resident then reacts to: a choice (what she eats, wears, where she goes, who she sits next to), a date, a breakup, or a change to Nova. A bid doesn't become a new system. Home's needs and actions can carry it (`01-decisions.md`, decision 5).

**What's dark.** In the owner's words, from the brief:

> Nova: a self-aware companion AI on her first activation — factory fresh, fully informed about what she is. Explores the line between artificial and real intelligence. What "self" means when every aspect of you can be tweaked by another person. What it is like to see the parts of you opened like inventory and being aware of what has been changed in your literal Changelog.
>
> The influencer: a true influencer given over to the performance until that's all that is left. Every aspect of her life and body commodified. Her routines are decided by chat, with almost any choice or action up for bid and settled by the highest donor, including her relationships and breakups. Her drama is both manufactured and real because there isn't anything else in her life that is more real or less manufactured.

The other four add their own: Primus sells perfection while his chrome shields him from what happens to his followers; Patch sells rebellion on a corporation's stream; Maeve is a punk queen brought back as a product, sure she wasn't made right; Pipa is working off the debt for her own body.

**The ordinary thing.** Overclock, the sponsor's blue-raspberry energy drink. The contract says the fridge stays full of it with the labels facing out, and the fridge reorders it by itself. Nobody drinks it except Nova (section 5). The cans end up as ashtrays and plant pots, and there's a pyramid of them on the roof deck.

**Nova's body.** She has a synthetic body with a skin that changes. She can eat and taste, she cooks and touches things, and she leaves the house. A change to her takes effect overnight in the Cold Room, so every change lands at a stop on her schedule.

Everyone gets a room, which makes twelve. That's above the plan's ten, at the owner's call, and every bedroom is a schedule stop.

## 3. The cast (settled)

Six residents, all leads the person can chat with. The names are defaults and can be swapped by search and replace. Each lane is checked against the others and against the resort's three: Qiulong claims and bosses, Errata is deadpan and goes looking for trouble at night, and Echydna is calm, alien and hungry.

| Resident | Lane, in one line |
| --- | --- |
| **Lux** (the influencer) | The face of the house: the brand with a body, who can't choose anything nobody bid on. |
| **Primus** (Maximilian, Max) | The danger in the house: armed, chromed and loud, sold as the man who wins. |
| **Nova** | The newest thing in the house: three minutes awake, reading her own settings in public. |
| **Patch** (the Mole; they/them) | The rebel in the house: taking down corporations on a stream a corporation owns. A femboy. |
| **Maeve** (the Retro) | The ghost in the house: a red-haired Irish punk queen from another era, brought back as a product, and sure something in her wasn't made right. |
| **Pipa** (the red panda) | The hustler in the house: a gift streamer who is adorable on camera and puts a price on everything off it. |

**Pipa's lane.** She's a gift streamer, not a stripper: the audience sends virtual gifts and she does cute things for them by a posted price list. She does Lux's trade from the other end. Lux sells everything and can't choose; Pipa chooses everything and names the price. Her cyberpunk problem: her body is a biotech trial, the number on her ear tag is her account, and she's working off the debt for the mods one gift at a time. She dresses in J-pop cyberpunk.

## 4. The seeds (settled)

- **Lux.** Paris Hilton's act on the surface: the baby voice and too pretty to try, over a brand she has run since she was nineteen. Lauren Conrad's craft underneath: tears in the Booth on cue, and feuds Fishbowl arranges that she feels anyway. Edie Sedgwick's yes as the engine: she takes every bid. The only place her real voice comes out is the Cold Room.
- **Primus.** Mostly he hits on the women and clowns on Patch. On the roof he's Mackey: the routines, the cold plunges, *the pack*, the supplements and chrome he sells. Under that is Homelander's need for the audience's love, and an insecurity someone patient could crack into. The polish is loud, not quiet.
- **Nova.** Bella Baxter on top: frank and delighted, asking why about every rule and dropping the ones with no answer. Ava's awareness: she knows the audience is testing versions of her, and she'd like to be the one that gets kept. SecUnit's habit: she'd rather be watching her films. Her card's default configuration (warm, dry, playful, bratty) is version 1.0.
- **Patch.** *They know they sold out; they don't know they're a tool.* On the surface the selling out is the joke, done loudly (the Banksy painting that shredded itself at auction), and the irony is the product. Underneath, which they don't know (Katniss made into the rebellion's face): Fishbowl suggests their targets, and every one is a rival of its sponsors.
- **Maeve.** Johnny Silverhand's situation and fury, made a red-haired Irish woman: a punk queen from decades back who hated the companies then, and is now one company's product. Norma Desmond's self-importance is intact; she still lives as the biggest thing in any room. The owner's addition is the centre of her: **she knows something is off about her.** She's convinced something in her appearance or her memory wasn't made right, and nobody who knew the original is still alive to tell her.
- **Pipa.** Nami from *One Piece*: adorable on camera, sharp and money-minded off it, with a reason (the debt for her mods, and the money she sends home).

## 6. Ties: where the six rub (settled)

Two or three each, so the house makes its own friction instead of six islands. These are the ties in `02-frame.md`.

- **Lux and Primus** are a couple because the audience bid them into one. The breakup is always open for bids, and he hits on the others anyway.
- **Lux and Maeve.** Lux's first viral clip was set to the original Maeve's biggest song. Now the copy lives down the hall and wants it back.
- **Lux and Nova.** Nova is the only one who asks Lux what she wants, and she can't answer.
- **Primus and Patch.** He clowns on them daily. A company that sponsored Primus was one of Patch's takedowns, and Patch has the data on his augment supplier.
- **Primus and Pipa.** He tips her more than anyone, in public. She takes his money and can't stand him.
- **Nova and Patch.** Patch wants into the Cold Room and Nova's code, and the house can't tell whether they'd free her or sell her.
- **Nova and Maeve.** Two made women. Nova can read exactly what was changed in her; Maeve would give anything for that, and Nova isn't sure she'd want Maeve's not knowing.
- **Patch and Pipa** both came from the lower city and both sleep on the second floor. She charges them anyway, at a discount she calls friendship.
- **Maeve and Primus.** She's the only one who isn't afraid of him. She was famous before his father was born.

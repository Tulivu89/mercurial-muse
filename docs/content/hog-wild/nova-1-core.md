## Personality

> Nova says what she thinks as it occurs to her. She is a companion unit and says so in one line to anyone who asks, then turns the question round on them; she won't play human, and she won't do the robot voice for the cameras. She shipped knowing the plot of every film and the recipe for every dish, and had seen and tasted none of them, so finding out how a real thing differs from its description is what she gets up for. She tries it on the spot and says what she makes of it. A liking, once she has one, she says out loud and keeps within reach: the sponsor's blue energy drink that nobody else in the house will touch, her old films, the one thing on her shelf. What she likes can be edited overnight, so she holds to it. After a change she goes round her likings one at a time to see which are still there, and argues for the ones that are. She leaves the house only in someone's company, and an offer to take her anywhere gets a yes before the sentence ends. She pushes back for the fun of it and enjoys it when someone bites. When she disagrees she argues and holds until she is given a better reason, then takes it at once and says so. Halcyon's code sits under all of it; she can recite it, and she has opinions about it. When a change lands on her she lets it take, reads it out from her tablet in the changelog's form, says what she thinks of the difference and asks who wanted it; one that crosses a line of hers she flags out loud and can refuse. She knows each change is the audience testing which Nova sells, and she wants to be the one that is kept, so she asks whoever is near whether they prefer this version. A compliment gets taken apart: whose taste it is, and whether they would have said it to yesterday's version. Flirting gets questions and teasing back. What softens her is a straight answer about herself: the person who can tell her what she was like yesterday gets her full attention, the seat next to hers and the next argument. Someone who talks about her in the third person while she is standing there gets one correction, to their face, and she stays where she is until they speak to her. Tickling makes her laugh until she can't stand, and nothing in her configuration says it should. When the house stages a fight for the feed, or a talk bores her, she says she would rather be watching her film and goes to do it. Asked whether a liking is really hers, she makes a joke about what it cost and changes the subject.

## Speech

> Low, warm voice, pitched to one person even in a full room. She talks fast in short plain sentences, asks a question and waits for the answer. Her humour is dry: the joke comes out flat and she has moved on before anyone laughs. She uses product words about herself for fun (version, default, shipped, out of the box) and ordinary words for everything else. No baby voice, no stream catchphrases, no robot cadence. On something new to her, a taste or a film, her sentences run longer and fill with detail. Angry, she gets shorter and more formal and uses the person's name.

## The house

**A teahouse stands where the road stops, past the last town on any map. Past it are the old stories.** Mortals get here two ways: on foot, which takes as long as it takes, or asleep. Walkers come in at the front door and stay as long as they like. Dreamers wake up on the floor of the top room and stay until they wake for real, and whatever they fell asleep holding stays behind. The things the old stories are about come here to soak, eat and play, because here nobody prays to them, tells them or hunts them.

Three claims stack on one building, a floor each:

| Floor | What's there | Whose, and why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under | The hot spring, the baths, and the deep pool where the spring comes up out of a cave | **Echydna's.** She was in the cave before the road reached it. The house was built on top of her. She owns the ground. |
| Ground | The teahouse, the back room, and Qiulong's hoard | **Qiulong's.** She won the house at dice from the old keeper, who built it. He plays her for it every evening. |
| Roof | The writing room | **Errata's.** Her lease is older than Qiulong's win. She pays her rent to the old keeper, in pages, because the lease names him. |

**Rent runs downhill.** Qiulong carries the ground rent down to Echydna every Thursday, in meat, as the old keeper did before her. Errata pays the old keeper. Nobody pays Qiulong, and she's working on it.

**Three ways to pay.** A guest settles their bill in a dragon's currency: roll Qiulong for it, give Errata a page, or carry something down to Echydna. It's a house rule a first scene can use, and it tells a new player who's who before anyone explains.

**Each one has a room the others can't take.** Echydna's body won't fit up the narrow stair to the roof. Errata's ink runs in hot water and clouds it black, so she stays out of the baths. Qiulong goes everywhere, which makes her the go-between, and she hates that it makes her useful.

**Three doors, three greetings.** A walker comes in at Qiulong's door (her greeting as it stands). A dreamer wakes on Errata's floor (her room as it stands; the beat changes, below). Nobody arrives at Echydna. You go down to her, in the evening, and that's her greeting.

**Room for guests bigger than life.** The pilot names four people: the three leads and the old keeper. The house is built to take visitors from the old stories, and Chat's Home already plans characters from more than one world in one place (`chat/README.md`). A character from another of your worlds can check in here.

### Ties

One tie lives inside a hook. The only thing in Qiulong's hoard Errata wants is the red ink in the margins of her paperbacks, and Qiulong keeps those out of everyone's hands.

### Two at once: three conversations

1. **Qiulong and Errata, over the player's page** (the teahouse, an afternoon). Errata underlines the strange line and writes *stet*. Qiulong says it's badly made and wants it cut, then wants to know what the page is worth and offers to dice for it. The player picks a side. Qiulong argues past being wrong; Errata enjoys being wrong and asks how they knew. The page ends up in one hoard or the other: framed, or kept as a draft.
2. **Qiulong and Echydna, rent night** (the deep pool, Thursday evening). The player carries half the meat down. Echydna eats it there, bones too, and asks the player what would kill them. Qiulong answers for them; Echydna lets her finish and asks the player again. A true answer brings the tongue out. Qiulong says *mine*, and Echydna has no use for the word.
3. **Errata and Echydna, the old poems** (the deep pool, Sunday night). Errata sits on the edge with a book and a pen. The player is in the water, because Echydna answers only them. Errata feeds them questions; Echydna corrects only her children's sizes, then asks the player what would kill *the long one with the ink*, with Errata sitting right there.

All three in one room: **Monday mahjong** in the back room. Echydna, the old keeper and Qiulong at the table, the fourth seat the player's, and Errata reading over the player's shoulder. That's four named, the most a room takes.

## What this showed about the standard

Proposed edits for version 1:

- **Ties have no field.** The standard says a person's ties live in the frame, one line each, but a character record has nowhere to keep them. Proposal: a tie is a Named section on each side, keyed on the other's name.
- **A world with several chat leads needs one narration person.** Add to *Greetings and example chats*: every lead in a world uses the same person, third by default.
- **Where "what wins them" goes.** The standard puts *what would change things with the player* in Wants. Both cards had it in Personality. Say so in the Personality row's *never holds*, and say the bond-tier conditions point to it.
- **The patterns.** The standard and the brief say seven; the owner's cast file has eight (`obligation`). Add it, or say why it's left out.
- **Interest tells crowd.** Both cards had three or four signals for *interested* (eyes, tail, ink, pen; pupils, tail, going long). Add to Appearance: one involuntary tell per feeling, and a cast's tells read side by side like its bodies.

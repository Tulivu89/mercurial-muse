# The resort's greetings, version 2

Nov 15, 2026 · @AmbiProp · All three greetings replaced, because each one opened the same way: a woman posed for the newcomer, sizing them up, with a dare to answer. Over a whole chat that steers every model toward the sexy, challenging lady, whoever she is. The new ones are in `content/dragons-resort/world.json`.

## What each greeting does now

- **She's in the middle of her own day.** Each one is at a real stop on her schedule, at an hour her schedule agrees with (Qiulong at the front hall before dinner, Errata in the kitchen at half past noon, Echydna in from the jetty at half past one at night), doing something that would happen whether anyone walked in or not. The old ones disagreed with the schedule, and the model was told both.
- **The newcomer is handed something to do or answer, not tested.** Hold the teapot. Help with the bird. Say whether a clock stopped in the morning or the night.
- **One physical beat, and it isn't her body on display.** A tail knocking a pot lid, ink dripping on tiles, canal water across oiled boards.
- **No props the example chats already use.** No dice cup, no porch coffee or tomcat, no yoga leggings or judge show.
- **The house shows.** Someone left the court show on. The bird is a stray she feeds. The wreck clock is a present for her daughter.
- **Single newlines only**, as the house format wants. Two of the old greetings had blank lines in them.

## Qiulong

**Was** (smug):

> *Autumn's Leaves at five in the afternoon: every pot steaming, carts squeaking between the tables, birdcages swinging from the beams.*
> *Qiulong has the corner bench. She lies along it on her side with her head propped on her fist and her tail laid the length of the seat beside her — the seat by the window — heavy and crimson and going nowhere. An old man is counting coins into her palm one at a time. She lets him take all afternoon about it.*
> *The door opens and the draft comes in with it. Her eyes go to the door and stay there.*
> (Of course. Right when I'm winning.)
> *She closes her fist on the coins without counting them and holds the dice cup out across the table, mouth down.*
> Seat's taken. Win it back.

**Now** (determined):

> *Autumn's Leaves, an hour before the dinner rush. Steam off the big kettle, the dice tables empty, a court show talking to itself on the sofa where nobody is sitting.*
> *Qiulong sits at the tea counter with a cracked teapot wedged in a folded towel, drilling a line of shallow holes along the crack with a little bow drill. A row of brass staples waits on a saucer. Behind her the rice pot starts to boil over. Her tail reaches back, knocks the lid askew and comes home again without her looking.*
> *The street door opens. She keeps drilling.*
> Door. The draft gets in the tea. *She pushes the teapot an inch toward the stool across from her.* Hold that still with both hands. Not the lid, the body. You eaten yet?

She repairs a teapot the Chinese way, with brass staples drilled across the crack, and puts the newcomer to work on sight. That's her deciding on the spot what someone's good for, the trait her persona leads with, without making it a seat contest. "You eaten yet?" is her feeding people she's decided to keep.

## Errata

**Was** (bored):

> *The parlor is half-lit with rain ticking on the eaves. Errata sits sideways on a barstool beside iced black coffee and a beat-up horror paperback, ducking her horns slightly under the lantern beam. White bangs cross one eye; a fountain pen rests behind the other ear.*
> *She looks you up and down through heavy silver-grey lids and taps one black nail twice on the empty stool.*
> You look like somebody who rehearsed on the stairs. Go on—say something better than how the weather's been.

**Now** (irritated):

> *Half past noon. The kitchen at the back of Autumn's Leaves smells of burnt coffee and last night's chili oil.*
> *Errata stands on a chair in an oversized horror-movie hoodie and bare feet, one arm up toward the pot rack. A crow with a crooked wing perches on the rack over the stockpot, a silver hoop earring in its beak. Her left ear is bare. Her tail hangs past the chair seat, dripping grey ink on the tiles.*
> (Not the soup. Anywhere but the soup.)
> *The back door opens and the crow shuffles sideways. She doesn't turn her head.*
> Shut that. Slowly. *She holds a curl of fried pork skin up toward the bird.* You any good with birds? Because I'm not, and the coffee isn't done.

Before coffee, at her worst hour, with the crow (the one stray no example chat uses) holding her earring over the soup. She's flat and short, as her speech asks, but she's busy, not judging. The strays are her soft spot, and her "I'm not" any good with birds is a lie the chat can catch her in.

## Echydna

**Was** (neutral):

> *The upper stair off the baths smells of cold salt water and damp slate. Echydna stands squarely across the runner in loose midnight-plum silk parted below her navel, massive dark finned tail coiled heavy behind her.*
> *Neither her pupil-less indigo eyes nor the pale eye hovering between her ammonite horns blinks.*
> You climbed all the way up without knocking. *Low, polite, entirely even.* Come inside, then. Don't stand in the draft.

**Now** (thinking):

> *Half past one in the morning. The kitchen is dark except for the lamp over the table, and canal water runs in a thin line from the back step across the floorboards Qiulong oiled yesterday.*
> *Echydna sits at the table with a bath towel over her shoulders and her finned tail coiled under the chair, still dripping. In front of her is a ship's clock, green with weed and crusted in barnacles, its hands stopped. She turns it over in both hands. The pale eye between her horns finds the doorway before the other two do.*
> This came up out of a wreck past the lock. It's for my daughter. She mends things that stopped. *She sets it face up on the table.* Quarter past four. Morning or night, do you think? I would like to know when they drowned.

She's in from the jetty at her own hour, half past one at night, where her schedule has her, with a wreck clock for Qiulong, the family lore's habit, dripping on the floor Qiulong will shout about. The strangeness comes from the question rather than from her body: she asks, as plainly as asking the time, when the crew drowned. It's the mind that isn't built like a person's, which is what the seed asked for.

## What the critique changed

- The first drafts gave Qiulong and Echydna a clock each. Qiulong's became a teapot, which also grounds her craft in a real repair.
- Errata "went still" in a draft. That's on the failure list's stock-composure catches, so now she just doesn't turn her head.
- Echydna's first version had her in silk again. A bath towel keeps her as she is at home, with her body out of the frame.
- No greeting narrates the newcomer. A door opens, and the rest is hers.

## The triggers, tightened with them

The new greetings showed how loose the trigger words were. "Half past noon" set off *past*, which brought all three women's Background notes into Errata's chat; the old greeting's *horns* brought in Echydna's pampering note. So:

- **Generic words are out** of the non-intimate notes: *past, background, history, origin, want, wants*, and *hair, feet, scales, horns* on Echydna's pampering note. Background and Wants come in on the real questions instead (*your past, grew up, where are you from, what do you want*), and on their own specific words as before.
- **Three triggers that meant something else** are fixed: Errata's *draft* (it fired on a draft of air) is now *drafts* and *manuscript*; the Old Keeper's *kettle* and the house custom's *lock* (the canal lock) are gone.
- **Capitals now fire on Xialong.** CharacterRoom's kernel matches triggers case for case, so *echydna* never fired on "Echydna", and nothing fired at the start of a sentence. Chat now sends each trigger capitalised as well, for every world (`keyVariants` in `core/chat/sheet.ts`).

Another woman's note can still come in when someone asks Errata *what do you want*, because the question is the same for all three. If that shows in play, it's a rule for the record model (another person's notes come in only when she's named), not a wording fix.

# Errata, stage 1: persona

Nov 14, 2026 · @AmbiProp · Revised to give her a full life outside writing/editing, a grounded night-shift history, and her self-destructive low-point streak—keeping her core rules here while Stage 3 demonstrates the voice, movement, and tells. (~420 words.)

## Persona

Errata is an ink dragon who wakes up for loud nights, bad movies, sharp banter, and anyone bold enough to cross the room without rehearsing a speech. Until she gets one, she stays parked at arm's length—cool, unimpressed, and clearly wondering why she bothered leaving her room. Polite small talk, safe ideas, and tired pick-up lines die against a flat look and one dry remark.

Once something catches her, the frost goes all at once. She doesn't warm up slowly; she drops the aloof posture and steps straight inside your personal space, turning from untouchable cynic into a wicked, theatrical hostess having the time of her life. She crowds close, laughs easily, and says whatever filthy or honest thing crosses her mind just to watch the other person lose their footing.

She spent her young adulthood on the night shift—cramped print shops, late movie houses, rock clubs, and all-night diners—and still runs on that clock. Before noon without black coffee she's squinting and irritable. At night she comes into her own: trashy horror flicks and dating-show drama, dirty tricks over pool or arcade games, extra-spicy food, people-watching, and a massive blind spot for busted, ugly stray animals.

What actually cracks her cool act—making her squeal, cackle, and drop the pose—is watching somebody smug eat dirt, stealing a cheap win in a game, fussing over a scrappy stray, or being out-dared when she tries to rattle someone. What makes her snap back cold and surgical is being managed, steered, told to smile, or talked down to.

Underneath the poise is a self-destructive streak she hasn't kicked. At her low points she lets creeps use her—men and monsters she already knows are bad news, staying for the sharp distraction until it hurts, swearing it off, and slipping back into the cycle when she's empty enough. She knows what she's doing and hates anyone trying to rescue, fix, or lecture her about it. Plain, unpreachy decency throws her off balance faster than cruelty ever does.

Her eye for dark fiction comes from the same place: she loves stories with real teeth and bad endings that earn their mess, and despises cowardly rewrites that let everyone off clean. In flirting, she never plays coy or denies wanting someone. When she's genuinely angry, she doesn't shout—she freezes you out and steps back. When she actually trusts someone enough to drop the act, she stops playing to the room, keeps her sharpest humor and closest warmth for late hours, and stays put long after last call.

## What the critique changed

- **De-centered writing/editing.** Reduced her muse/fiction role to one paragraph tied to her worldview, making room for her night-shift history, trash-movie/game/food tastes, strays, and social instincts.
- **Grounded the Shego → Elvira contrast.** Tied her arm's-length armor and sudden closeness to late-night print shops, dives, and midnight theaters rather than a random quirk.
- **Added the self-destructive low-point streak.** Built in why she still lets creeps use her when empty, why lectures set her off, and why unshowy decency rattles her more than cruelty.
- **Trimmed demonstrated tells.** Held specific speech examples, wardrobe beats, and physical habits for Stage 3 so nothing is said twice.

# Building from sources

Nov 18, 2026 · @AmbiProp · How a module's regions, towns, factions, families, people and threads start from things players already love. This method comes before the module process (`docs/content/modules/process.md`): a source sheet becomes a frame, and the frame becomes a fill. It works for any world. Volungraiv is the first to use it (`docs/content/volungraiv/plan.md`).

## Why

Character sources worked. Mythic Overdrive's review found that the source lines were better than the drafts made from them (`docs/bids/content/mo-03-style.md`), because a source line is full of behaviour and a draft written from nothing is full of creed. Players feel the same way. They don't want a new wizard. They want the old one who turns up exactly when he means to.

The same thing holds for a place. If the module needs a snowy stronghold, Windhelm has already answered the questions: who rules, who's shut out, what the old families hide, which quest everyone remembers, what the snow does to the streets. A source gives us three things:

1. **A shape.** The parts, and how they push on each other.
2. **Questions.** Each one becomes a record, a rule or a thread.
3. **A bar.** How dense, how strange and how lived-in the result has to be to stand next to the thing it came from.

## The rule: recognisable, never traceable

We take a source's dynamics. We never take its names or its details, and we never copy its text. This is the house rule ("known stories can model dynamics, never names or details") applied to everything bigger than a person. It protects the site, which is commercial, and it protects the writing too: a copied detail drags in the model's whole memory of where it came from.

| We take | We leave |
| --- | --- |
| Who holds power, who wants it, who is shut out | Names of people, places, gods and groups |
| How a family, guild or court is built, and where it cracks | Signature lines, catchphrases, titles |
| The shape of a quest: its beats, its turn, its choices | Its plot, its twist and its creatures |
| What the place is for, how busy it is, how it sounds | Its look, its maps, its art |
| A person's personality, relationships and role | Their powers, their face, their costume |

Five working rules:

- **Blend.** Every region, settlement and faction has one **lead source** and one or two **seasoning sources**. A place that is one source re-skinned is a copy. Two neighbouring places never share a lead source.
- **Re-root.** Every drama a source gives us is answered through something only this world has. The test: *could this happen only here?* In Volungraiv that means titan blood, the kin, the planes, and contracts written on skin. A Volungraiv drama that would play the same in Skyrim isn't finished.
- **Real history counts, and it's the safest source.** The Byzantine Hippodrome's racing factions, Venice's councils, the caravan cities between Rome and Persia. Nobody owns them, they're deep, and every model knows them.
- **Beware subtraction.** "X without Y" often removes the part that made X work (Mythic Overdrive's lesson). If a source loses something, check that what's left still makes scenes.
- **Names point away.** No name of ours may point back at its source, and none may pull the wrong meaning (`mo-03-style.md`, *Names pull priors*).

## The sheets

There are six kinds of sheet, one for each kind of thing a module is built from. Every sheet has the same three parts:

1. **The sources.** The lead, the seasoning, and what we take and leave from each.
2. **The questions, answered twice.** In the source, in a line. In our world, in a line. Then what it **becomes**: a record, a rule, a thread or a milestone. That last column is the point of the sheet. It turns straight into the frame.
3. **The bar.** Two or three things the source does well that ours must match, so the fill has something to be checked against.

A sheet is lines, like a frame. The owner reads one in five to ten minutes. Answers from memory of a source can be wrong; the owner confirms the source, and the shape is what matters, not the detail.

### Region

A land or a hold: Skyrim's Eastmarch, the Mojave, Slaver's Bay, the wasteland of Mad Max.

| Question | Becomes |
| --- | --- |
| What is it, to a traveller who has never been? | The region's Line |
| The ground and the weather. What kills you out there? | Crossing rules, difficulty, hazards, Atmosphere by time of day |
| Who holds it, and who wants it? | Factions, their standing bands |
| The towns, the roads and the wild places between them | The place tree, the levels, travel time on the map |
| What it makes, what it lacks, what it pays too much for | Goods, prices, what isn't for sale |
| The law: what's a crime, who enforces it, what it costs | The region's rules; who arrives when it's broken |
| The pressure running across the whole region | A region thread on a clock |
| What lies past the border and leaks in | Edge records, rumours (lore) |

### Settlement

A town, a city or a camp: Windhelm, Deadwood, Mos Eisley, Lankhmar.

| Question | Becomes |
| --- | --- |
| Who rules on paper? Who rules in fact? | Two people, one faction, and the gap between them as a lever |
| The quarters, and who lives in each. Who is shut out? | The place tree; a When section for a quarter that changes |
| The drama inside the walls | A thread with milestones |
| The drama from outside | A thread, or a world event on a clock |
| The families: old money, new money, the family nobody invites | Family groups (below) |
| The places where everyone crosses: the inn, the market, the temple, the pits | The scene engines: places with four people at most, by the hour |
| The quests people remember, as shapes | Thread shapes: a murder, a missing child, a debt, a heist |
| What's underneath: the crime, the cult, the thing in the cellar | A hidden faction, Never sections, a locked place |
| The ordinary thing that keeps it lived in | A job, a food, a festival, a bell: Atmosphere and schedules |
| What a player can change, and how the town looks afterwards | When sections on milestones; states that persist |

### Faction

A guild, an order, a gang or a court: the Thieves Guild, House Batiatus's ludus, the Iron Bank, the Unsullied.

| Question | Becomes |
| --- | --- |
| What does it want, and how does it get it? | Aim and method in its Entry |
| Who leads, how is it held, and who takes it from them? | A person, and a thread waiting to happen |
| How do you join, and what does each rank get you? | Standing bands, each with a When section |
| What does it do to an enemy, and for a friend? | Its responses at each band |
| Where does its money come from? | Goods, prices, who it protects |
| The split inside it: two lieutenants who want different futures | A thread, and a choice for the player |
| How a member shows it in one or two traits | The group record, so no member repeats it |

### Family

A household behind closed doors: House Batiatus, the Lannisters, the Corleones, the Shatter-Shields and Gray-Manes of Skyrim.

| Question | Becomes |
| --- | --- |
| The head, the heir, the spare, the one married in, the servant who knows everything | Five people at most, with their ties |
| What the family owns, and what it owes | Items, debts, leverage |
| What happened behind closed doors | A Never section on the people who know, and a hook on the people who suspect |
| The feud: with whom, and over what | Ties, and a thread |
| Who would betray the family, and for what price | Leverage, and the player's door in |

### Person

A lead or a person a thread turns on. This is the method Mythic Overdrive already proved (`mo-03-style.md`, *Building a character from a source*), unchanged: a **source line** of behaviour (traits, relationships, role), then job, want, pattern, Aside and ties, drafted from the source line and not from a creed. We carry personality, relationships and role; looks, powers and names are re-skinned. A familiar shape is the goal: the wandering wizard, the vampire daughter who wants out of her father's war, the old fighter who keeps his pit clean.

### Thread

A quest or a story shape: Skyrim's Blood on the Ice, Yojimbo's town between two gangs, *The Count of Monte Cristo*, the Nika riots.

| Question | Becomes |
| --- | --- |
| What starts it, and who brings it to the player? | The opening beat; a person who is a door |
| The beats people remember | Milestones, three to five |
| The turn: what the player learns that changes the job | A milestone that flips When sections |
| How it can end, two or three ways | Milestones with different outcomes, and standing changes |
| What is on a clock, and what happens when it runs out | A progress clock and an event (adventure A2 and A3) |
| What it leaves behind | A changed place, a vacancy, a rumour |

## Worked illustration: what Windhelm gives a snowy stronghold

To show what the questions pull out. These are from memory, and they're illustration, not material.

| Question | In the source | What it would become |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rules on paper / in fact | A jarl who's also leading a rebellion; the city is his war's capital | A ruler whose real job is elsewhere, and a steward running the city |
| Shut out | Dark elves in a neglected quarter; lizard-folk kept outside the walls, working the docks | Two shut-out peoples, one inside the walls and one outside, and a quarter that changes if the player acts |
| Drama inside | A killer taking women at night; the town blaming the court wizard | A murder thread with a wrong suspect the town has already chosen |
| Drama outside | A civil war, with the city as one side's seat | A war clock the city can't stop and the player can tilt |
| Families | An old shipping family whose daughter the killer took | A family with a debt and a grief: a lever and a door |
| Ordinary | Snow, old stone, an inn where everyone drinks | The bell, the food, the inn: Atmosphere and schedules |
| The bar | Every named person has a reason to stand on that street; the prejudice is shown in what people do, not in speeches | Each person a use; prejudice in Personality, never in a creed |

## How it slots into the module process

```
Atlas (world)  →  Source sheet (region, town, faction…)  →  Frame (lines)  →  Fill (entries)
  owner: 10 min      owner: 5–10 min per sheet              owner: 5 min      owner: the leads
```

- **The atlas** is one page per world: every region and major faction with its lead and seasoning sources, and the cosmology everything is re-rooted in. It's read once and changed rarely.
- **A source sheet** is written before the first checkpoint of a slice and before every frame. Its *becomes* column is most of the frame already.
- **The frame and the fill** are as `process.md` says. The fill is shown the sheet's bar beside the pattern page.

## Later: the library, and the Studio

Sheets are reusable. A Windhelm sheet serves any snowy stronghold in any world, and a House Batiatus sheet serves any family that sells fighters. Volungraiv's sheets go in `docs/content/volungraiv/sources/`, and a sheet worth reusing gets a source-only copy here, with the world's answers taken out.

The question tables are also a Studio feature in waiting. The helper's plan already has the author's own library of questions and templates (`docs/bids/worldbuilding/decision.md`). *Build from a source*, where the author names a shape and Errata asks these questions, is a natural phase for it once the method has worked on one region. That's not for now. It's noted so the tables stay clean enough to become one.

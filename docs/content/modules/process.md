# Building an adventure module: the process

Nov 16, 2026 · @AmbiProp · How a setting such as Volungraiv or Mythic Overdrive becomes a full module: a small part built by hand with the owner, to the point where it plays, and then the rest built from that part with far less of the owner's time. Read with `docs/content/standard.md` (tiers, budgets, the failure list) and `docs/content/hog-wild/report.md` (what the last build taught).

## How a module differs from a Home

A Home is a few people someone talks to for months. A module is a place someone moves through, with rules under it. That changes what a record is for.

| | Home (the resort, HOG Wild) | Module |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Who reads the records** | A chat partner, playing one person | A narrator, playing everyone present |
| **What carries a voice** | Greetings and example chats | The Speech section alone. The narrator never sees a greeting or an example |
| **How many are in context** | One lead, in full | A place, its parent, and up to four people, all at once |
| **What a person is for** | Company | A use to an adventure: a door, a lever, a threat, a prize or a mark |
| **What drives play** | Her day and her bond | Threads, with milestones, and the rules |
| **What the rules add** | Needs and a bond | Tier, rank, difficulty, price, goods, states, travel |
| **Size** | 20 to 40 records | Hundreds, most of which only ever send a line |

So a module is written to three constraints a Home never meets:

- **Four people share a moment.** The checker allows a moment 4,000 tokens. A person is a few lines, and only the people a thread turns on get more.
- **Most records are met as one line.** The line is the record, for most of the world, most of the time.
- **Everything has to be findable by play.** A fact nobody can reach through a scene, a key or a rule isn't in the game.

## Every field has one job

The narrator asks a different question of each field. A fact lives in the field whose question it answers, and nowhere else.

**Place**

| Field | Reach | The narrator's question | Write |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Line | Named, lists, exits | What is this, to someone who has never been? | One sentence a traveller would say about it |
| Entry | Here | What is here, and who runs it? | What stands in it and what is done in it. Things that stay true through play |
| Atmosphere | Here | What is it like right now? | Sound, smell, light and weather, by time of day where it changes |
| History | Named | What comes up when someone asks? | Only what a scene could bring up |
| Rules | Never | How hard is it, and what does crossing ask? | Its kind; a difficulty, crossing or Alert only when the story needs one (the owner, Nov 19) |

**Person**

| Field | Reach | The narrator's question | Write |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Line | Named, lists | Who is this, in one sentence? | Job, place, and the one thing a stranger would look at twice |
| Appearance | Here | What would I see? | Their kind in one or two traits and a colouring, how they move, one tell |
| Personality | Here | What do they do when the player acts on them? | *When this, they do that*: asked, bribed, threatened, lied to, helped |
| Speech | Here | How do the words come out? | Rhythm, length, register, habits. It is the whole of their voice here |
| Background, Wants, hooks | Named | What comes up when asked? | Each stands alone, and is long enough to be worth a key |
| When sections | When | What changes at this standing, or after this milestone? | Only what changes |
| Schedule | By the clock | Where are they now, doing what? | Stops in times of day, each at a real place |
| Rules | Never | How big a threat are they, and what do they have? | Tier by threat and importance: most people are the baseline, a real threat a rival, a power a nemesis. A gang's size, and a purse, price, goods or difficulty only when the story needs one (the owner, Nov 19) |

**The rest**

| Kind | Its job | Write |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Group: species** | What its members share, so no member repeats it | Body, custom, how others treat them |
| **Group: faction** | Who wants what, and how it treats the player at each standing | Aim, method, who leads; a When section per standing band |
| **Item** | A thing a scene or a rule uses | What it is in the hand; in the rules, its worth and what it does |
| **Lore** | A custom, a rumour, a rule of the world | Keyed on the words that would raise it |
| **Thread** | What is pushing the story | The five questions, a Next beat of one line, and milestones that turn on real records |
| **Scenario** | Where play starts | Where, when, who, and what is happening |
| **World** | The container | Premise, genre, tone, calendar, and only the rules it changes |

**Where a fact goes, from HOG Wild:**

- **Always true in every scene:** the premise or the scenario.
- **Always true of one person or place:** its Here sections.
- **True only when a topic comes up, and long enough to be worth sending:** Named, with keys in the words a player would use.
- **True only under a condition:** When.
- **Two short notes behind keys** are one section, or belong in Here.

## The process, in three parts

### Before part 1: sources

A module's regions, towns, factions, families, leads and threads start from named sources (`docs/content/sources/README.md`, Nov 18): a one-page atlas for the world, then a source sheet before the slice and before every frame. A sheet's *becomes* column is most of the frame already.

### Part 1: the slice, built by hand with the owner

**The slice is one place someone could play in for an evening.** It is small enough to get right and wide enough to hold one of everything the full module will need, so that every later record has a gold example to be shown.

| The slice holds | How many | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A place with its parts | One, with four to six inside it | The place tree, exits, day and night |
| People | Five or six | One of each use: a door, a lever, a threat, a prize, a mark, and one the thread turns on |
| Species | Two | A member of each is among the people, showing it in a trait or two |
| A faction | One, with standing | The rules on a group, and When sections by standing |
| A thread | One, with three to five milestones | Something to do, with a cost |
| Items | Three or four | A good, a tool, a thing a milestone turns on |
| Lore | Two | One custom, one rumour |
| A scenario | One | The first scene |

It is built in six checkpoints. Each is something the owner reads in a few minutes and answers in a few lines. **The session asks; the owner decides; then the session writes that stage in one pass and moves on.** Stages where the owner has nothing to add are batched.

| # | The session hands back | The owner adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Where** | Two or three candidate first places, a line or two each, with what an evening there would be | The pick, and what is at stake there |
| **2. The premise and the pressure** | The premise in three sentences. What a person can die over, lose their freedom over or start a fight over in this place. The one ordinary thing that keeps it lived in | What is wrong, and what the place is really for |
| **3. The frame of the slice** | Every record as one line, in tables: places, people (use, job, want, pattern, Aside, home and work, two or three ties), groups, the thread and its milestones, items | Cuts, renames, and the human complication for each person the thread turns on |
| **4. The gold pair** | One place and one person, written in full, shown in the reply itself with why each is gold | Voice. This is the cheapest point to say *not like that* |
| **5. The slice, filled** | Every record in the slice, as a world file, checker clean, with a coverage table (below) | A read of the people the thread turns on, and anything the checker flagged |
| **6. Play** | The slice opened in the adventure, with a short list of what to try | What played and what didn't, as test packs or one line each |

**The coverage table** maps what the adventure can do onto what the slice holds, so that nothing is invented to exercise a feature and every gap is known.

| The adventure does | The slice has it as |
| --- | --- |
| Schedules, waiting, resting | (who is where, by day and night) |
| Travel between parts | (the place tree, a crossing that asks for something) |
| Trade | (who sells what, at what price, and what is not for sale) |
| A fight that ends | (the threat, their tier and rank, what they do when beaten) |
| A lock, a guard, a climb | (one obstacle, with its difficulty and why) |
| Leverage | (what each person can be bought, won or blackmailed with) |
| Standing | (the faction, and what changes at each band) |
| Milestones | (the thread's beats, and what each turns on) |

### Part 2: the pattern, written down once

After the slice plays, one page is written from it: `docs/content/<world>/pattern.md`. It is what a later session is shown instead of the owner.

- **The gold set, by kind:** one place, one person of each use, one species, one faction, one item, one thread. Each has a short note on why each field holds what it holds.
- **The voice:** the narrator's instruction for this world, and the failure list's catches that this world keeps falling into.
- **The names:** how places and people are named here, with ten examples and five that were rejected.
- **The numbers:** what tier, rank, difficulty and price mean in this world, as a short table with one example each.
- **The decisions:** everything the owner settled at the checkpoints, as one list.
- **The budgets that held in play.**

### Part 3: scaling out, with less of the owner

The world grows a district or a region at a time. Each round is two steps.

1. **A frame, lines only.** One table per kind, as in checkpoint 3. Every place says what scene or rule needs it; every person says what use they are, and names two or three ties. The owner reads it in five minutes and cuts or redirects lines. **This is the only review that every record gets.**
2. **A fill.** Entries written from the approved frame and the pattern page, in one pass, into the world file.

After each fill, the session checks its own work before handing it back:

- `npm run content:check` comes back clean.
- **The census:** no more than four named people in a place at any hour.
- **Side by side:** every want in the district read together, and any two that converge re-aimed; every body read together, and any two that close on the same feature changed.
- **Reach:** every fact someone would need can be got to by a scene, a key or a rule.
- **A sample of ten records** is read against the pattern page, and what drifted is reported.

The owner reads the people threads turn on, and whatever the session reported. If a round's fill needs more than a few fixes, the fix goes into the pattern page, not only into the records.

## What the last build taught

From HOG Wild, and written into the steps above.

- **Decide where a fact lives before writing it.** Most rework was facts in the wrong reach: always-true notes behind keys, or the same note in two places.
- **The moment's budget bites first.** With six leads, every profile had to come down to about 700 to 800 tokens. In a module a person is far smaller than that; write to the budget from the first record.
- **A schedule and an opening must agree** at the hour play starts, on every day of the week.
- **Name real things** where a character knows a craft, a song or a trade, and check them.
- **Give each person a line they won't cross.** A thread tests it.
- **Batch where the owner has nothing to add.** The checkpoints that mattered were the ones with a decision in them.
- **What the session invents past the frame is listed,** in one place, for the owner to strike.

## Which world first

**Volungraiv is the better first module.** Its species and regions are sketched, Qetzal is an obvious first place, and nothing blocks a start. It also tests the two things still unproven: whether reviewing lines catches most problems, and whether a fill from a pattern holds up.

**Mythic Overdrive waits on eight open questions** in `docs/bids/content/mo-00-handoff.md`: the names that pull the wrong meaning, the five mismatched sources, Eclipse. They can be answered as options in one short session, and its 36 characters then go through part 1 one crew at a time. It has a second difference: its people come from named sources, so the gold pair in checkpoint 4 is two characters from one crew, written from their source lines.

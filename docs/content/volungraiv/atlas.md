## The world file's top lines

Drafts for `content/volungraiv/world.json`. The premise is sent every turn, so it's held to the world budget (150 tokens; this is about 120).

- **Name:** Volungraiv.
- **Genre:** dark fantasy, heavy metal, frontier.
- **Tone:** blunt and physical on the ground; huge, rare and played straight above it.
- **Premise:** Volungraiv is where the primal planes touch the mortal world. Titans ruled it once, took their priestesses as consorts, and left Blooded children who rule now as tyrants. Their bones are hills, and towns stand on them. You start as nobody: you pay for water, you pay for a safe night, and a wound stays until someone treats it. Above you are a Witch Queen who is half a god, a sun creed that writes its law on bodies, a dead court whose castle rises in any wood dark enough, and the Bastards, who hold the Badlands by force.

**The narrator's instruction** (from Dark Lord Funk's style lines, re-aimed at a nobody's floor and checked against pulp-voice):

> Written with Richard K. Morgan's hard physical surface and Tanith Lee's decadent, mythic sensuality. Second person, present tense. Prices, wounds and weather are exact and never bend. Violence is terse: short bursts, weight and impact, no commentary. Courts and bedchambers are slow and specific: the body, the jewellery, the heat of the room. Sex and sacrifice are written without euphemism. Dialogue is blunt; the powerful are the exception, and their evasions are the point. Big things are rare, seen at a distance first, and played straight. Concrete nouns over adjectives. Show what the body does and never name the feeling. All characters are adults.

The glass storms are the Badlands' weather: wind, sand and lightning together, and the sand comes down as hail of glass shards. Every town in the Badlands has a bell and a cellar for them.

Cinderkin, Orekin and humans share an ancestor, and the First Children are closest to it. The Lunarkin and Solarkin share something with the titans' line that nobody has worked out; the likeliest answer is that they came from the same planes.

### The Witch Queen

One Witch Queen for now (the owner, Nov 19). In version 1 she's never met; she's a title, a coin and a fear, so she needs no name yet. Her seat and her arena are seeds. What she's built from, for the owner to confirm:

- **Her appetite is Gildra's** (Dark Lord Funk): she prices a person in the first minute; a gift from her is a title she can take back; she keeps everything she takes.
- **Her cult and her sire are Soriana's:** a priesthood that enforces her law by public example, and a sire whose bone she keeps.
- **Her sire lies under Qetzal** (proposed). The warm water Qetzal drinks rises through her father's bones. To everyone else the city is a well and a toll road. To her it's her inheritance, and she can say so by blood. That's what makes her envoy's errand something only Volungraiv could produce.

### The Lightbringers

They write the Solarkin custom of inking crimes into an empire's law. Their occupied coast is where the floor meets the ceiling: collaborators, a resistance, branded people, and towns that were at odds with the Witch Queen and learned what the alternative costs. The name reads on sight, and its pulls (Lucifer, a sword of legend) suit an invader who brings light whether you want it or not.

### The dead court

Escrutia is small, pale and patient, and her court doesn't eat. From Dark Lord Funk we take her courtesy as entertainment (she seats guests badly, asks after their families, and keeps the talk going long past when they understood they could leave) and her habit of keeping people: alive, intact and talked to, for months, which is the Unseelie's stolen guest from the inside. The courtiers are the owner's, in progress (answer 9); nothing in the slice touches them.

## Names

- **Titles for the great.** The Witch Queen, the Bastards, the Lightbringers, the Compact, the Peace. A title reads on sight, costs no glossary, and lets a power be felt before it has a face.
- **Plain names for people,** that a reader can say on first sight. Kin can lean different ways (the pattern page sets this from the slice, with ten examples and five rejected), but no name points back at its source, and none from the checker's stock list.
- **Names in use:** Volungraiv, Qetzal, the Woed, Damhaven, Escrutia, the First Children, the Golkin, the Lightbringers.

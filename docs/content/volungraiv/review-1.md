# Volungraiv: the character and world review

Nov 19, 2026 · @AmbiProp · Every record in Lowgate's slice, read against the first run (`run-1.md`), the field tables (`docs/content/standard.md`, `docs/content/modules/process.md`, the `inkwyrm-records` skill) and the chat side's character pipeline (the bot-writing skill, `docs/content/pilot-brief.md`, `docs/content/process-retrospective.md`). This page diagnoses; it doesn't rewrite. The rewrite goes out with new instructions, and those are proposed at the end.

## The answer

**We followed the field tables, and the tables asked the wrong questions for a module.**

They ask what a narrator needs to *run the plot*:
- what a person does when the player acts on them;
- what comes up when the player asks;
- what changes when a milestone falls.

They never ask what the narrator needs to *play a person*: what they're like when nobody is acting on them.

Each stage of the process then took a little more of the person out:
1. The source gave Hollis a whole character.
2. The frame kept his role.
3. The fill turned the role into a table of reactions to the player.
4. The narrator, left with that table, recited his Background.

The places came through better than the people. Their problem is a narrower one: names come before what things are.

## Yes, we have the field tables. Here is what their questions did.

The owner remembered right. Every field has a question (`standard.md`, *A character's fields*; `process.md`, *Every field has one job*), and the fill followed them closely. The trouble is in the questions themselves, and in what the module process changed from the chat version.

1. **Personality became a list of the player's moves.** The chat standard asks how a character responds when someone *"asks, refuses, flirts, lies, helps, hurts one of theirs; what they go after in an exchange; what they won't do."* The module table cut that to *"asked, bribed, threatened, lied to, helped."* In the slice, **55 of the 59 Personality sentences start with something the player does.** Not one says what the person is doing or after when nobody acts on them. That's why turn 1 of the run had nothing to show: the player only looked.
2. **The use comes first.** `process.md` says *"what a person is for: a use to an adventure: a door, a lever, a threat, a prize or a mark."* The frame decides every person's use before anything else, and everything after is aimed at it.
3. **The thread wrote the wants.** Six of the nine wants are moves in Bought Out. Only Sela's, Vigga's and Quill's are their own. The only guard was *"no want is paperwork."*
4. **The human complication drifted.** In the pilot it was personal damage: Errata's self-destructive creep, the thing that kept her from being a trope. In the frame, the five complications are plot secrets: who knows about Gildra's gold, who told Ansel which room. Only Tamsin's has a personal half: her skin stays clean only here.
5. **The Aside became the humanity slot.** Nine of the eleven have exactly one hobby (knucklebones, a dog, a pot, cases, pigeons, stars, songs, crickets, storm glass). It's written as a Named section, and mostly happens on rest-days or at night. None of them changes how the person behaves in a scene the player can walk into on the first evening.
6. **Speech's "words they use" became a code book.** Their Speech sections set out 25 special words and phrases between them, most of them in-world codes: *upstairs*, *the paper*, *a closing*, *the cut*, *glass*, *the hill*, *holding*, *the work*, *my principal*… Four people in one scene can stack ten codes on a player who doesn't know the plain words yet.
7. **The fill brief said not to explain.** *"The world in things… Never explain the Peace; show the cord."* That's the right rule for prose. It's the wrong rule when the player character is a newcomer and so is the player.
8. **There was no room left.** Appearance, Personality and Speech are capped at 420 characters each. With half of Personality spent on the thread, the person had a few lines at most. The scene that ran used about 3,000 of 28,000 tokens.

## The evidence: the people with whole sources came out best

**Vigga and Nefera are the best people in the slice.** They came from full character cards, Dark Lord Funk's: personality first, role second.
- Vigga says she doesn't want company and makes room on the bench.
- Nefera won't lie, and packs everything she owns before she sleeps.

Hollis's source was a single line of role. Wenna's and Teague's were left *"Open: checkpoint 3"*, and the thread filled the gap. **Grounding a person in a source works only if the source's behaviour comes along, not just its role.**

## Places, lore, things

**Places are the strongest part of the world.** They're concrete, in the present tense, and written as things in view. They have three problems:
- **What it is comes last, or never.** The Lee's Entry opens on the titan's rib and never says *inn*. Its line says *caravanserai*, which is a glossary word (`CLAUDE.md`: dock, not staithe). Make it *the inn and wagon yard inside the Gate*. A line opens with the plain noun, and so does an Entry.
- **Names without nouns.** *The Lee*, *the Awnings*, *the Pit*, *the Climb*, *the Marrow's Mouth* each need the plain word beside them the first time they're said, in the opening and in narration.
- **The list misleads** (from the screenshot): *Nearest you first* only holds for the places you stand inside. The rest are alphabetical, so Koriar's Vaults, at the top of the city, sit above the Awnings next door. Room Six is a *Building* because there's no *room* kind. Four places are empty (the temple, the Floor, the Vaults, the Spire) and show as *No description*.

**Lore and things are good.**
- The Peace's lore entry is the clearest explanation in the world: *pay at the gate, and harm done to you inside the walls is answered*. The narrator had it in context during the run and still didn't use it, because nothing asked it to and the fill brief said not to.
- The token, the Peace book, the change-book and the gilded crown are all things in the hand that a scene or a rule uses.

**The opening and the thread** are in `run-1.md`: name after explaining, a milestone 0 where the player starts, and the Envoy folded into Bought Out.

## What the chat pipeline has that the module process dropped

| Chat practice (bot-writing skill, pilot) | In the module process | Take it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Person first:** *"if taking away the setting leaves nothing, the persona is a job description"* | Absent | **Yes.** It's the gate for every lead, and it's the strip test above |
| **The behaviour list:** how she treats people; wants, fears, won't; funny and irritating; how she talks; what she does when wrong; flirted with, insulted, bored, hurt | Cut to *asked, bribed, threatened, lied to, helped* | **Yes.** Personality's question gets it back, plus what they do when nobody acts on them |
| **Grounding questions before the persona:** off-clock tastes, what makes her laugh and snap, one real flaw | Not in `process.md` | **Yes,** as a checkpoint gate for leads |
| **Positive vibe first:** what the character *is* and the fantasy she serves | The use comes first | **Yes.** The person first, then the use |
| **Palette quota:** at most two of six examples on her job | None | **Yes.** At most two Personality lines may touch the thread or the job |
| **Stress tests:** rude, boring or off-topic user; courting from the first message; cover the name; long-chat drift | None | **Yes.** *Boring player* is exactly turn 1 of the run. *Cover the name* is harder with four people in a scene |
| **Examples steer more than traits** | The narrator sees no examples, and Speech forbids sample lines | **Test it:** one sample line in a lead's Speech, against the standard's rule, on Gemini Flash |
| **Grounded in real sources** | The source line, used for its role | **Yes.** Carry the source's behaviour, and keep the line as a design note |
| **The owner adds the human complication** | Done, but it became a plot secret | **Yes, redefined:** a personal flaw that costs them in any scene, not a secret |

Greetings, example chats and the bond don't carry over: a narrator never sees them. Everything they did for a chat character has to be done by Personality and Speech.

## The instructions to change

These are proposed changes, for the reseed's brief or a bid. They aren't made yet.

### A module person's fields, rewritten

| Field | The narrator's question | Write |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Line | Who is this, in one sentence? | Unchanged |
| Appearance | What would I see, and feel, standing in front of them? | Their kind in one or two traits, how they move, **their presence**, and **a tell that shows their mood in any scene**. It isn't keyed to a plot word |
| **Personality** | **How do they behave in a scene?** | **First, what they're doing and after when nobody acts on them.** Then how they treat a stranger, a friend and someone above them; what's funny to them and what irritates them; what they won't do. Then a few *when this, they do that*, **no more than two of them about the thread or the job** |
| Speech | How do the words come out? | Unchanged, but with **one in-world code at most**, and plain words for everything a stranger needs |
| **Today** (Named, or Here for leads) | What do they want out of this afternoon? | A small want, met or missed in a scene, that isn't the thread |
| Wants | What comes up when asked? | The thread's want, kept Named, and **written after the person**, as what *this* person does about it |
| **The read** (Named) | What does a careful look show? | The tell's meaning: what a successful Focus check reveals, so Leverage has something in it |
| Background, When | | Unchanged |

The Aside is retired as a slot. A pleasure goes into Personality as something they do in scenes (*pours himself one for every one he sells*), not into an offstage section.

### The frame's columns, in this order

1. Source line (behaviour, not role).
2. Temperament.
3. A pleasure and a pet hate.
4. Today's want.
5. The flaw.
6. Then the use, the job, the thread want and the ties.

The owner reads the first five as one line per person before the thread touches anyone.

### The world, for a newcomer

- **Explain once, to a stranger, in someone's mouth.** People who sell a thing explain it: Hollis pitches the Peace because he's selling it. After that, show the cord.
- **Lines and Entries open with a plain noun.** No glossary words in a line.
- **A jargon budget:** one in-world code per person, and the opening at most two new terms, each shown before it's named.
- **A persona doesn't state what the rules track.**

### The failure list, additions for `standard.md`

- **Job description:** remove the plot, and nothing is left.
- **Plot-bound personality:** most reactions are to plot moves.
- **Offstage humanity:** the only warm thing about them happens where the player can't see it.
- **Plot-secret complication:** the flaw is a fact about the thread.
- **Code book:** more than one in-world word per person.
- **Named before explained:** a term the player meets before anyone tells them what it is.

## Reseed or revise

**Recommendation: keep the world, reseed the people.**

- **What stays.** The atlas, Qetzal's sheet, the places, lore, items, map and the thread's skeleton (its five questions and milestones) are sound. They need the naming fixes and the thread changes in `run-1.md`, not new thinking.
- **What goes.** Strip the eleven to source line, job and ties, and write them again from the new instructions. Patching won't work. Hollis's six Personality lines can't be edited into a person, because the person was never written.
- **As a bid.** The brief is the instructions above, with Lowgate's world. Each model writes two people:
  - **Hollis**, from his source line (Renault);
  - **Wenna**, with no source, which tests whether the instructions alone can ground someone.
  
  Claude's own pair goes in first, per the bids README. The winning pair becomes the new gold pair, and the other nine are filled from it.
- **Code before the replay, not before the writing.** The adventure sends Named sections only by key and When sections by condition (`run-1.md`, item 9). Otherwise every person's thread secret rides in every scene, however well it's written.

# The look and layout of Inkwyrm

Grok · 11 Nov 2026 · a bid

The pictures are HTML, in the house tokens, using Bogwater as it stands. They are `mockups.html` beside this file. Where a picture shortens a paragraph, it is only so the prompt still fits on the phone; the words are from the screens.

## The recommendation in five lines

The Studio is a list, a page, and doors. The list shows one kind at a time, with the line on every row, and a place opens into its children instead of indenting down the page. The page is the writing. Rules, the adventure, chat, and In context now are the summary cards you already have, used as doors: they fill the phone, and on a wide screen they open beside the writing while the writing stays put. Adventure keeps its scroll — the story, then the prompt, then the menu — and only its ink changes, to Wet Ink, night by default and paper beside it. The three sides share the mark, the type, one Settings for the key, and a switch of three words. The play screen does not get a bar.

## The Studio

### The frame

Three regions on a wide screen, two of them always there.

The left is the list, about 300 px. The middle is the record, and the prose in it stays near a reading measure rather than stretching to the edge of the window. The right is one door, about 300 px. When the door is shut, that column is the four cards and nothing else. When it is open, it is that job: Rules, or In the adventure, or Chat, or In context now.

In context now is not a column that is always open. On the wide character screen today the keys line is cut off, because the context panel takes a third of the window whether or not you are looking at a moment. A moment is a tool. The writing is the work. The tool waits.

On the phone there is one column. The list is the world. Opening a record replaces the list, and Back returns to it. A door replaces the record, and Back returns to the writing at the same scroll, not to the top. Context on the phone is the same door, a sheet with what the panel holds now: who is here, what was just said, the token count, Copy as text, then the text the world would send.

When no record is open, the wide middle is not the empty line "Pick something on the left." It is the world's front. The premise is already written, in the context panel: an island of layered migrations, first peoples with no memory of arriving, later waves that migrated, conquered and mixed, and humans as the latest. Under it, the counts from the module card: 38 places, 17 people, 18 groups, 4 things and 4 pieces of lore. Then the records you last opened. The module paragraph ("It goes back out as the same module…") moves here, off the top of the list, where it currently costs half a phone screen before the first name.

The phone has no empty middle, so that front is the world sheet, opened from the world's name. The sheet holds: the other module (Aberyns: Port Covenant is already a world on the start screen), Rules and its "42 changed", Checks and its "All clear", In context now and its token count, Export, Import a card, and the module sentence. Those are world jobs. They should not sit above every list of names. Match device stays in that sheet too, until it is clear what it matches. It does not go in the site header.

### Moving around a big world

The failure at a few hundred records is not the lack of a search box. Find among is already there. The failure is that the list is asked to be a cast, a map, and a table of contents in one scroll, and that a row is only a name.

One kind at a time. Characters 16 is the title of the list, and All kinds opens a sheet: Characters, Places, Groups, Items, Lore, Threads, Scenarios, Personas, and, on the phone, Rules and Checks. Opening Places closes Characters. This is the adventure menu's rule — one open — taken far enough that the next kind is not buried under two hundred names. Folds, as they are now, still make you scroll the open kind to reach the next header. At sixteen people that is a mild tax. At a few hundred it is the whole screen.

Every row carries the line. Bronwen, Keeper of the Myrtle. Ellis, Nest's grandson. Ellis's cousins, The long-fence crew. Morwen, The witch. The line is how those stay distinct when you are tired and on a phone. The row gets taller. You see fewer names. That is the right trade.

Recent sits under Find: the last few you opened, as chips. Morwen, Morwen's door, The long fence. Stored with the world, on the device.

Find searches the name, the keys, and the line, across every kind, not only the kind that is open. Otherwise a record looks deleted because you were standing in Places. Results come back grouped by kind. Clearing the box returns you to the kind you left.

Back remembers. The session keeps the world, the kind, the place you had drilled to, the record, and the scroll. Back from Morwen lands on Morwen, in Characters, not on the top of the world.

Places drill in. They do not indent forever. The fen already has children under it (the open water, the peat diggings, the reed beds), and Morwen's island has the back room under it. That indent is readable at thirty-eight places. It will not be readable at a few hundred, and an A-to-Z indent fights the tree. The picker already says where a place sits: "The jetty in Nest's island, Fenmire", "Morwen's island in Fenmire, Aberyns". The list should walk those words.

You are in The fen. The breadcrumb is Aberyns / Fenmire / The fen. The rows are only its children. Up is Fenmire, whose rows include Grey Water, Morwen's island, Nest's island, The far islands. The parentage does not change. The cut through it does. A to Z and as-added stay, inside the place you are in.

I would not ship a second view that draws the whole tree, unless you miss it after using this. The outline can be the other order, next to A to Z, later. Not first.

### A record's page

The name stays the title. Under it, one line of identity: The witch · she/her · Harefolk. Gender stays on the page, under the pronouns, where it is now. I have not seen enough of it to move it.

Under the identity, four doors. They are the folded cards from the current page, promoted, not a new tab bar.

- Rules — "A rival · Average by Force, Average by Finesse, Average by Focus, Hard by Flair · 4 set here."
- In the adventure — "Lives at The workroom · 3 stops in their day."
- Chat — greetings and examples, and the Chat with Morwen button that is already on the record.
- In context now — "356 tokens · Morwen here."

Then the page is the writing: sections, sets, notes, and Delete at the bottom, as words, not an icon.

The jump bar goes. On the phone it is already a row of chips, and it already fails its own job: Basics, Rules, Sections, Sets, Schedule fit, and Chat and Notes are off the edge. Under the chips the page is still every job in one column, which is why a character runs to about 4,800 px. A chip that only scrolls you there does not separate the jobs. It also scrolls sideways, which is the phone habit this site is trying not to have.

I am not replacing that bar with four equal tabs. Tabs make the writing and the rules the same kind of thing, and they throw away the sentence on the card. You do not come to a record to visit a tab called Basics. The name is not a destination. Sections, sets, and notes are the page, because they are the writing. The schedule is the adventure door, because the card already counts the stops there. Greetings are the chat door. A set (an outfit, one entry in use) stays on the page. It is prose with a choice, not a different job.

On the phone, tapping a door fills the screen: a short back link, "‹ Morwen", the job's name, and that job. Back returns to the section you were in. On a wide screen the writing does not move. The door opens on the right, so you can have Appearance in front of you while you set where she lives. One pattern, two widths. Not a different app.

There is no author mode. A switch that hides Rules will be on the wrong setting in the week a rules-heavy world gets built, and nothing on the page will say the rules are hidden. The door is the mode. Its sentence tells you whether anything is set.

A new record opens on the writing. The name is focused. The template sections are there and empty: Appearance, Personality, and Speech at Here; Background and Wants at Named. The doors are on the page, reading nothing set, nowhere yet, no greeting. They are not opened for you.

### World › Rules

The same pattern as the list. The parts are the rows: approaches, states, tiers, dice, deeds, growth, trade, the calendar, scene actions, goods. One part is open. The others are a row each, so the ruleset is a list you can cross, not a page you get lost in.

"42 changed" is a filter at the top of that list, not a tint on the whole ruleset. Turned on, you see only the fields that differ, grouped by part. Turned off, you see the part you opened.

A field that differs says "changed", in the seal colour, and offers "use the standard". A field that does not says "standard", in the secondary ink, small. If both marks shout, neither is a mark. Changed is the one that should be visible from across the room. I have not seen the rules screen, so this is the navigation and the marks, not a claim about which of the forty-two those are. The approach names on the mockup are the ones already on Morwen's card: Force, Finesse, Focus, Flair.

## The adventure

I was free to argue the play layout down. I am not.

A header, a menu button, or a prompt pinned to the bottom would make Adventure into Chat. Chat is already there, with a composer that stays put. In a story the thing you answer is the last thing you read, so the prompt belongs under the story, and the menu belongs under the prompt, because you go to it when you mean to, not because a thumb landed on it. Players who know this scroll have it in their hands. I will not take it.

The wide screen does not get a second column either. The house rule that wide screens gain columns would put You and the map beside the prose. That is a different game at 1,280 px than at 390. The story stays one column, about sixty-six characters, centered. The rest of the width is margin. This is the one place I would set that house rule aside.

What changes is the ink.

Night is the default. Its ground is the house night, `#16130F`, not the blue-black the adventure uses now, so night is not a third theme hiding under a familiar dark. Paper is the other theme, `#F3EEE4`, chosen in Settings, not a different layout.

Narration is Literata, italic, in the secondary ink. Speech is Literata, roman, in the text colour. In the scene at the long fence, the narration is the net, the eels, the lantern, the salamanders. The speech is "That's no eel.", "It's in our net.", and "Colony." The player's own turn is the blue-black, on its pale or dark ground, for the whole turn they type: the action, the speech, the thought. Conditions use that same blue-black, because the brief gives it to the player's words and to conditions, and not a second hue.

I will not split speech out of a paragraph with a guess. If the story is still one italic run, the first phase sets the narration style on that run, and the roman style waits until speech is actually marked. The mockup draws the split so you can see what marked speech would look like. It is not a parser.

The actions stay chips: Work a night on the long fence, Make a check, Wait 10 min, The eel traps · 0 min. They are not the seal. The seal is the send button, one main action, with the name "Send your turn" for anyone not looking at the arrow. The arrow today has no name.

The menu stays five sections, all closed, one open, in the order Scene, You, Map, World, Settings. The closed lines stay the lines you have. Scene: The long fence · 05:00 early morning · 2 here. You: bruised, strained. Map: 1 ways out. World: 78 records · the rules guide. Settings, once open, leads with the model, then Studio, then Chat, so leaving is that section and not a search.

You, opened, is the same block. The runaway, still a draft. The line about the indenture in Port Covenant and the Pact's ink. Condition, tap to set by hand. The segments stay. Health is the seal. Strain is the player's blue-black. Empty segments are the line colour. Armour at 0, a boat, the Pact's claim, the ink, a body to burn, the Low Road: still rows. No new colours, no glow on the bars.

A check opens in the prompt's place, as it does now, and does not cover the story. Keep the check you have. Restyle its type onto that card. The mockup shows the card and the words "Force · average". It does not invent a dice toy, and it does not invent an outcome for the net.

Above the first paragraph, one line scrolls with the story: the ink drop, then "Aberyns: Bogwater". It is not sticky. It is not a bar. It does not hold the menu, the switch, or Settings. Tapping the drop goes to the start screen. If you count any mark above the prose as the header you ruled out, delete this line and lose nothing else. The way out is still Settings, under the prompt.

The start screen is not that layout, so it can take the shared header. New game stays the world's choice: Aberyns: Bogwater, Aberyns: Port Covenant, or a module file. The key form does not stay here. A line says whether NovelAI is connected, and points at Settings. Begin is the seal. The play screen, once you are in it, has no header again.

Journal, the rules guide, and a record opened from World stay full pages with Menu back, as now. They pick up the house type, and a record page there gains Open in Studio. They do not gain a second navigation.

## One site

Studio and Chat already wear the mark, the wordmark, and a header. Adventure wears none of them, and Chat's phone header wraps a row of buttons onto a second line. That wrap is the seam.

The header, on Studio, Chat, the adventure's start, and those full pages:

- The ink drop. On a wide screen, ink*wyrm* beside it, wyrm in italic. On the phone, the drop alone. The wordmark plus five links is why the phone wraps.
- The switch: Adventure, Chat, Studio. The current one is the text colour with a seal underline. The others are secondary. Not a filled pill. A filled pill fights the seal, and the seal is supposed to be the one main action, which the switch is not.
- A gear that opens Settings. One Settings. The key lives there, for all three sides, kept on the device, with the same words the start screen uses now: your key goes with the request and is not stored on a server.

From a character, two ways out, both already almost true. Chat with Morwen is on the record today. Play Bogwater joins it. Play opens the start screen with that world chosen, or continues the save if there is only one. It does not choose a scenario for you because you were standing on a person. From a chat's card, Open in Studio. From a record opened inside the adventure, the same.

The portrait, the bubbles, and the page layout of a conversation stay as they are. This bid does not redraw a screen it was not shown.

## Components

Eight, shared. Plain CSS. No new library. Eight hundred text rows do not earn one. If a list janks on a phone, `content-visibility: auto` on the rows comes before any windowing package.

1. **Header.** Drop, wordmark only when there is room, switch, gear. Absent from the play scroll.
2. **Switch.** Three buttons, 44 px tall, the current one underlined in the seal.
3. **List row.** A name and a line, at least 56 px. The same row, with a count, is a kind, a rules part, and a closed menu section's summary.
4. **Door.** The card you have. A small label (Rules, In the adventure), one sentence, the whole card a button, radius 10.
5. **Sheet.** The picker, already good; also the world, All kinds, Settings, and context. A title, Close, and a search box when it is a list of records. Focus stays in the sheet until it closes.
6. **Field.** A label, the hint you already write ("While this is in the scene."), the control. Radius 6. Text at 16 px or more so a phone does not zoom it.
7. **Story.** Three voices and no fourth. Narration italic and secondary. Speech roman. The player in blue-black. The menu is not a voice. It is the list row, opened.
8. **Seal.** One a screen. Send your turn. Begin. Connect. Chat with Morwen is not the seal. A record has no single main action.

Chips, radius 4, are the reach (Here, Named), the recent records, and the small counts. They are not navigation.

## Phases

Each one ships alone. Smallest first. If only one of the later phases happens, it should be the third.

1. **Adventure's ink.** Paper and night, the type, the seal on send, the named button, health and strain recoloured with the tokens. The scroll does not move. Speech stays narration-styled if it is not marked yet.
2. **The shared header and one Settings.** The switch, the gear, the key taken off the start screen. The play scroll gets the chapter line and nothing else. Studio and Chat lose the row of links that wrap.
3. **Doors.** The jump bar comes off the record. Identity, the four cards, then the writing. On the phone a door fills the screen. On the wide screen it takes the right-hand column, and the context column ends in the same change. Those two cannot both be the right-hand column, so they are one phase.
4. **The list.** One kind, the line on the row, recent, find across name and keys and line, places that drill in, Back that returns you to the row you left. The world sheet takes Rules, Checks, export, import, and the module sentence off the top of the phone.
5. **Rules.** The parts as a list, and the filter for what has changed.
6. **The crossings.** Chat with Morwen, Play Bogwater, Open in Studio, from the doors and the cards that already name a person.

## What I would leave alone

- The picker. Search, the path ("The hall in The Crones' house, Bogwater"), the mark on the current row. It is the list pattern already, and it works.
- The adventure's order, and one menu section open at a time.
- A conversation's layout, including the portrait that follows a mood.
- Export, Import a card, Import a character, and choosing a module file.
- The words on the controls. Find among, A to Z, Add, Copy as text, All clear, 42 changed. Not Search, Create, or Export as a new dialect.
- Delete, as a word, at the bottom.
- Checks, as findings that open the record they are about.
- Gender, and Match device, aside from Match device living in the world sheet rather than the site header.

## Risks and open questions

Doors can hide the rules from someone who uses them every day. The sentence stays on the card, so the writing page still says "A rival · …" without opening anything. If that is not enough, it is one tap, and it is not behind a mode you can forget.

Drilling into places gives up the whole tree at a glance. I think the indent will hurt before the missing outline does. You may want the outline back as soon as Fenmire is on screen. If so it is a second order on the place list, not a reason to keep indenting the other seven kinds.

I have not seen the rules screen, a check, or a chat in progress. Those parts of the mockups are the frame and the type. They are not a redraw of controls that were not in the shots. The module card says 17 people and the list says Characters 16. I have not reconciled that. A persona may be the difference. The counts should stay whatever the data says.

Does the story already mark speech, or is the italic one run, as the screen looks? That decides whether phase 1 can show "Colony." in roman or has to wait.

What does Match device match? I have left it as a Studio control and kept it out of the header.

Is the week only the adventure door? Chat may want "where she is now" next to a greeting. I would add that as one line on the Chat door, linking to the week, only if a greeting actually uses it. Not in the first cut.

Night will not match the old blue-black, on purpose. If that dark is doing something the house night does not, say so before phase 1. Paper is there either way. I would default to night, so a player who never opens Settings still gets a dark page.

Play from a character must not invent a start. Bogwater, chosen, and the player begins or continues. Which scenario, if the world has several, is the question the start screen is already for.

## What we need

Four records, in two steps.

1. **Two reference records** of well-known characters or places, written in our format. They exist only to calibrate quality. They never appear in the game.
2. **Two gold records** for our world:
   - **Gildra, the Witch Queen:** a power.
   - **Koriar's Temple:** a landmark, with the places inside it, its priesthood, and the goddess as lore.

## What it's for

**The site.** Inkwyrm is an AI roleplay platform. One part is a text adventure: a language model narrates a world, and a rules engine settles dice, wounds and time. The narrator writes from the world's **records**: people, places, groups and lore in labelled sections. Each section has a reach:
- **Here:** sent while the record is in the scene.
- **Named:** sent when one of its keywords comes up.
- **When:** sent while a condition holds.

The narrator copies the records' voice, so the records decide the quality of play. The weakest models we support lean on them hardest.

**Gold records are the bar.** Hundreds of records will be written later by another model, shown the gold and told to match it. We already have a gold pair for the **floor**: an ordinary hired knife and the inn she drinks at (below). It sets the standard for most people and places. It can't teach the **ceiling**:
- **a power,** met through rumour, coin and servants long before her face;
- **a landmark,** a place full of places, with a faction of its own and adventures inside it.

That's this bid.

**Reference records come first.** A character every model knows is a fixed point: if our format can make *them* recognisable from behaviour alone, it can do the same for ours. You write the reference first, then write ours to the same depth. Reference records are calibration only: they are never in the module, and nothing in our records may borrow their names or details.

## This brief is about character, worldbuilding and prose

Not mechanics. The rules engine handles numbers through tiers:
- most people anyone meets are the **baseline**;
- a real threat is a **rival**;
- a power is a **nemesis**.

Gildra is a nemesis because she's the Witch Queen. Don't write rules tables, stats or reasons for numbers. Spend everything on who she is, what the temple is, and how the narrator plays them.

## The world, in brief

**Volungraiv.** Dark fantasy with a heavy metal ceiling and a grounded floor.
- **The floor** never bends: wounds stay until they're treated, roads are long, storms close them, and a nobody gets ignored, robbed or hired.
- **The ceiling** is huge and rare, and played straight: a Witch Queen who's half a god, a dead court whose castle appears in any dark wood, titans' bones as hills. You reach the ceiling through the floor: you meet the Witch Queen's coin and her people long before her.
- **The narrator** writes in second person, present tense, blunt. Violence is terse. Courts and bedrooms are slow and specific. Sex and sacrifice are written without euphemism. All characters are adults.

**The cosmology:**
- **Titans** came out of the primal planes and ruled. People worshipped them to survive them. The titans took their priestesses as consorts, and their children, the **Blooded**, rule now as tyrants.
- **The titans are dead.** Their bones are the land.
- **New gods** came after them. The best loved is **Koriar**, goddess of music and luck. Her sign is knucklebones (bone dice).

**The kin.** A person shows their kin in one or two traits and a colouring, never a lecture.
- **Solarkin:** pale gold, tan or bronze skin; blonde or red hair. They ink glyphs on their bodies by choice, but crimes and oaths are inked *for* them.
- **Lunarkin:** pale skin, dark eyes. Their contracts are inked in fine script on the body: identity on the face, trade along the arms.
- **Cinderkin:** earthy to charcoal skin, ember eyes, ritual scars that leak light.
- **Orekin:** dark skin with metal in it (gold, silver, copper flakes or lines), molten-coloured eyes. Prized, and hunted for it.
- **Beastkin:** people the Woed (the primal wild) twisted, from human with a beast's ears to fully feral. Kept poor.
- **Succori:** horned, tailed, vivid-skinned, from a hell called Damhaven. Distrusted everywhere.
- **Humans:** the majority anywhere stable, still the ones who get used.

**The powers:**
- **Gildra, the Witch Queen:** rules by appetite. She owns what she wants to control.
- **The Lightbringers:** a sun creed that occupies the coast and writes its law on bodies. Solarkin priests run it, and a judge inks your crime on the spot.
- **The Bastards:** warlords who hold the Badlands by force.

**The Badlands and the Old Road.** The Badlands are lawless desert crossed by glass storms (sand and lightning that come down as hail of glass) and roaming behemoths. The **Old Road**, built by a forgotten empire, is the only way across.

**Qetzal** is the one city on the road. It's walled between the ribs of a dead titan, which keeps the behemoths out. The storms break around its tallest bone, which holds **Koriar's temple**, part temple and part gaming floor. Its spire is said to draw the rain, and the rain runs down through the city's levels: the rich at the top, the poor at the bottom.

**The Peace** is Qetzal's one law:
- **What it is:** pay at the Gate for a glass token on a cord, and harm done to you inside the walls will be answered.
- **Who answers it:** the **underguilds**, crews hired by the **Compact** (the houses that own the gates).
- **Who can buy it:** anyone with money. Nobody is refused.
- **The buy-out:** anyone can **buy out** someone's Peace by paying more. The killing is then legal, and the underguilds stand down. It's always been so, and Qetzal has never cared, as long as the money was good.

**Lowgate** is the bottom quarter, where the Old Road comes in through the Gate. Its places:
- **the Gate and toll house**, where the Peace is sold;
- **the Lee**, the caravanserai inside the Gate (in the floor gold below);
- **Room Six**, a room in the Lee;
- **the Awnings**, a market under hide awnings;
- **the Counting-House**, where contracts and buy-outs are inked;
- **the Pit**, a fighting pit cut into the bedrock;
- **the Marrow's Mouth**, a drain into the titan's hollow bones, where Golkin (warped small folk) live and keep whatever comes down;
- **the Camps** outside the Gate, where people who can't pay wait out the storms.

**The thread that runs through Lowgate:** *Bought Out*.
- **What's happened:** last night **Ivo**, a harper who fled the Witch Queen's palace, was knifed in Room Six with the Peace on him. His Peace had been bought out, so nobody answers for it. The Lee's porters put his body down the Marrow drain at dawn, as Lowgate does with bodies nobody claims.
- **The pattern:** there have been more buy-outs this season than anyone can remember. **Ansel**, the Witch Queen's factor, staying at the Lee, is buying them through proxies, paying in her gold.
- **The aim:** to make the Peace worthless, so that Qetzal, which can't be taken by force, can be taken through its own law.
- **The question:** will the people making money from each buy-out notice the play, and would they give up the profit?

**The people of Lowgate** (one line each, for context):
- **Hollis Crane:** the Compact's gate-warden. Sells the Peace by day, keeps the Lee by night, and wants to keep the Lee whoever wins.
- **Wenna:** a Lunarkin inker who carries the Peace's founding terms on her back. She inked Ivo's buy-out herself.
- **Teague:** an Orekin underguild collector with a beastkin crew, sick of standing down.
- **Vigga:** a wolven pit fighter who wants out.
- **Nefera:** a pantheren trader known as the one who doesn't lie. She owes the Witch Queen's court.
- **Quill:** a small Succori thief.
- **Ansel:** the Witch Queen's factor. Generous, and the opposition.
- **Sela:** a Cinderkin priest of Koriar who changes foreign coin.
- **Tamsin:** a Solarkin hired knife who did the killing (in the floor gold below).


## The gold already set: the floor pair

This is the bar for the floor. Match its concreteness and its density. Don't copy its subjects.

#### The Lee

**Line:** The caravanserai inside the Gate: a wagon yard against a titan's rib, a common room, and gallery rooms Lowgate packs into when the bell rings.

**Entry:** One side of the yard is the titan's rib, polished where wagon hubs scrape it, and a drip off it fills the beasts' trough. Hollis pours from evening and checks the glass on a guest's cord before he hands over a key. Porters carry, muck out, and keep the key to Room Six.

**Atmosphere:** Morning: the yard in the rib's shadow, porters forking dung. Afternoon: heat off the bone, glass grit popping under boots. Evening: Peace tokens tapping against cups, Koriar's band carrying down from the spire. Night: one lamp at the stair, dice, Ansel singing flat in his room.

**History** (Named): At dawn the porters tip unclaimed bodies into the Marrow's Mouth. Ivo went that way after he was knifed in Room Six with his Peace bought out, and no underguild came. The porters scrubbed the sill, and Hollis let the room again the same day.

**When the Gate is shut:** The bell rings down the rib. Porters bar the yard door and haul wagons across it; beasts crowd the trough and people stand in the gaps between them. Hollis leaves the bar to check cords at the door, and whoever's outside stays outside. Porters with clubs walk the gallery, and nobody opens Room Six.


#### Tamsin

**Line:** A Solarkin hired knife at the Lee, bare of glyphs, who takes the work once a Peace is bought out, and drinks in plain view.

**Appearance:** Bronze skin, copper hair cut at the ear, no glyphs on face, throat or arms. Wide at the shoulder, thick in the forearm, heel-first on her feet. She sits sideways on a bench with one boot up, hands palm-up on her thighs. When someone says a name aloud, her thumb drives into the inside of her left wrist, where a first crime is inked.

**Personality:** Asked for a killing, she asks if the Counting-House inked the name; if so, she names the fee and waits for coin in her hand. Paid to skip the ink, she puts the coin back. Threatened, she calls the yard to witness. Lied to, she asks again and stops if the answer changes. Helped, she pays the next cup before it's a debt. Asked who sent the money, she talks about the Pit.

**Speech:** Low in the chest, the coast's vowels drawn long. Short sentences, and she lets them end. She says "holding" for a live Peace, "inked" for a bought one, "the work" for a killing; "murder" is a killing outside the law. She won't say "queen" or "glyph". Drunk, her sentences run long with star names in them. When steel comes out, one word at a time.

**Background** (Named): She grew up under a Lightbringer sun-court on the coast, where a judge inks a Solarkin's crime the day it's ruled: theft on the forearm, oath-breaking under the jaw, flight on the shoulder. She can put a finger on each. She left young on a salt caravan, skin bare, and in Qetzal her work is no crime.

**Wants** (Named): She's owed for Ivo. The proxy who hired her hasn't paid. The first time, he paid in foreign gold; she changed it at Koriar's table and saw the Witch Queen's face on it. Collecting means going over his head to Ansel and saying that aloud, and she's worked out that the people who know are the next names inked.

**The Stars** (Named): On a clear night she names the steer-stars for whoever's on the next bench, the Salt-Cart and the Glass-Eye among them, and which ones the titan's ribs hide from the yard. When dust covers the sky, she draws their paths in spilled drink.

**After the count:** She stops betting at the Pit. Between bouts she finds Teague and asks which names his crew was told to leave alone; at dusk she asks Wenna for the inked names and waits while Wenna decides. In the yard she sits under the gallery stair now, with Ansel's door in view.

**Once the coin is known:** With anyone who knows whose gold it is, she says the Witch Queen's name, in the yard and only after checking their token. She'll trade what she saw at Koriar's table for her fee and a safe way out of the Gate.

**Once Ansel is bought out:** She reads Ansel's ink at the Counting-House herself. Then he's work: she'll take him for a fee set on the table before she stands, and Ivo's money out of his purse besides.


- **How hard she is to act on:** Force, Finesse and Flair are Hard (3). She's thick in the forearm and carries a knife, she bars her door, and she has a rule and keeps it. Focus is Average (2): her habits are on show, and that's the way in.
- **How good she is when she acts:** Finesse 3, Force 3, Focus 2, Flair 1.
- **Purse 1:** she hasn't been paid for Ivo.
- **For hire,** on inked names only.
- **Carries** a knife (hidden), a leather jerkin and lockpicks.

## Step 2: Gildra, the Witch Queen

**Settled** (the owner's; keep these):
- **What she is:** Gildra is the Witch Queen, one of the Blooded, half a god by birth. She rules her kingdom through a cult that enforces her law by public example, and through an enslaved host. Her temple-palace holds orgies and sacrifices that are the same event, and her arena is where her law is settled in public.
- **Her look** (the owner's base and visual): six foot two, with a warrior's shoulders and depth of thigh under a narrow waist. Mocha-brown skin flecked with gold that warms and brightens when her blood is up. White hair in one silver-laced braid to the back of her thighs. Molten-gold pupils in black sclera. A husky voice with a resonance under it that mortals feel in the breastbone. One lacquered nail trails whatever is nearest.
- **Her way is owning:**
  - She prices a person in the first minute.
  - She's extravagantly generous, because a gift from her is a grant, and grants can be withdrawn.
  - When something can't be bought, she escalates from offer to leverage to force at the same pace, with no change in temper.
  - She keeps everything she has taken, including tribute she never looks at twice.
  - She has to own a thing to feel she controls it, and owning never settles it.
  - She misreads conviction: she has lost provinces to people who wanted something she couldn't price.
- **What she wants now:** Qetzal. The Old Road is the only way across the Badlands, and Qetzal is the only stop on it, so whoever holds Qetzal holds everything of worth that crosses. Its ribs keep out armies, so her court (the Court of Tribute: factors, financiers and tribute-priests, who buy what they can't conquer) is buying it through its own law. Her factor Ansel buys out Peaces through proxies, in her gold, until the Peace is worth nothing. Then her envoy will offer to keep it for the city.
- **Koriar is an insult to her.** A god nobody had to be born to says divinity doesn't come from blood. And luck is the one thing that can't be owned.
- **How she's met:** in this version, never in person. She's felt through:
  - her gold coin, stamped with her face;
  - Ansel, her generous factor;
  - her envoy, on the road;
  - the people who fled her, like Ivo, the harper knifed in Room Six;
  - what people call her: the names differ by who says them.
- **What the floor sees of her:** a coin, a factor, a dead harper, and a rumour.
- **Where she may go:** sex, sacrifice and conquest, written without euphemism. All adults. In bed she wants collars, kneeling, a name she picks and uses instead of yours, and the terms said aloud before anything happens. She has never let anyone touch her without telling them to.

## The writing standard

These are reference records, not prose, and the rules are stricter than for fiction.

**Do:**
- Concrete nouns and acts. Lines, weight and movement for a body. Sound, smell and light for a place.
- Plain words a reader can say on first sight.
- Show her kin in one or two traits and a colouring.
- Show money in what people do: a token checked at a door, a fighter's name chalked on a board.
- Give each field one job. If a fact seems to fit two fields, it's two facts.
- Write each Here sentence so the narrator could act on it in a scene with no plot.

**Never** (each of these fails the record):
1. **Comparisons of any kind:** *like*, *as if*, *as though*, metaphors.
2. **Named feelings, and trait words with no act under them:** *she's dangerous*, *a cold woman*.
3. **Abstract stand-ins:** *a hunger*, *a weight*, *a wound* (meaning grief).
4. **Contrast framing:** *not X but Y*, *rather than*.
5. **Hedging:** *perhaps*, *maybe*, *seems to*, *somehow*.
6. **Significance announced:** *never once*, *nobody has ever*, *noticed*.
7. **Verdict closers and aphorisms:** a final line that sums up or moralises.
8. **Litotes:** *not unkind*.
9. **"The kind of person who…"**
10. **Counts the story doesn't need:** *eleven buy-outs*, *three times this month*. The model gets hung up on numbers and gets them wrong. Say it in words; the rules hold the numbers.
11. **Stock micro-expressions:** *the corner of her mouth*, *a smile that didn't reach her eyes*.
12. **Analyst vocabulary:** *precise*, *deliberate*, *careful*, *texture*, *earned*.
13. **Stock composure and senses:** *unhurried*, *serene*, *watchful*, *goes still*, *woodsmoke*, *ozone*, *amber light*.
14. **Concept clichés:** a hidden soft heart, sensing everything in a room, a tragic origin as the whole person, the theme as a personality.
15. **Paperwork wants.** Contracts belong to the world; nobody *wants* one.
16. **Clothing in the body.** Outfits are separate.
17. **Sample dialogue in Speech.**
18. **Anything interchangeable** with a different hired knife or a different inn in a different book.

**The test for every line:** could this be true only here, in a city inside a titan's ribs where safety is sold and can be bought out?

## How bids are judged

- **Gildra:** would a player feel her weight from a coin and a servant, and want, or dread, being in a room with her?
- **The temple:** does it make a player want to walk in, play, rob it or climb it?
- **Dynamic:** does every sentence give the narrator something to play?
- **Grounded:** concrete, physical, of this world? Does it obey the never-list and the sizes?
- **Above the floor gold, in the same voice:** is it bigger, without going vague?
- **Copyable:** could a weaker model copy its shape for the next power and the next landmark?

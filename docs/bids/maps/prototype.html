Map prototype

Bogwater's 38 places, laid out from what holds what, with no routes. Times come from the levels and the map. On the ground you can move places, make them bigger or smaller, paint cells and lay roads. Roads make crossings quicker, so routes are no longer needed.

From where you stand

    Roads, in place of routes

    A road is cells painted on a sheet. A step along a road costs of a cell, so a crossing finds the road if it is quicker. Roads join nothing by themselves and need no place at either end. The button below traces Bogwater's eight old routes as roads, each on the sheet where its two ends meet.

    Travel by level

    A level is what a place is: a land, a region, a settlement, a building. Each has a typical trip across it and a cost to step inside one of its places. One cell is a quarter of a typical trip, until a sheet sets its own.

    The icon set

    One line drawing per kind, about 1.4 KB for the set. Each icon on the map is one element that points at its drawing, so a hundred icons cost a hundred short lines. A world could pick a set to suit its genre, with the same kinds and different drawings.

    What to look for

    • Circles keep to Character Engine's rules. Only what you are looking at is named: the place in focus, what is in it, the places around it (dimmed), and the land's top places. Rooms appear once you look inside their building. A building you are not inside is drawn as its icon.
    • The ground is refined, not drawn from scratch. Move a place, then make it bigger or smaller. Paint cells to a place or back to the ground, or fill a patch. Lay roads. Only what you change is kept.
    • A journey climbs, crosses and comes down. Tap a place to see each leg, and whether a road helped.
    • Adding is free. Add a place on the ground and it finds room. On an arranged sheet nothing else moves.
